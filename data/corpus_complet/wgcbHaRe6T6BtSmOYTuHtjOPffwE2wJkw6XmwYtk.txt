General Introduction
In an era defined by rapid digital transformation, cybersecurity has transcended its role as
a purely technical safeguard to become a cornerstone of global economic stability. As cyber
threats grow in sophistication, governments and international bodies have responded with
a stringent wave of regulations. The European Union, through the European Parliament,
has been particularly aggressive in setting global standards, enforcing frameworks such as
the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) [1] and the Digital Operational Resilience
Act (DORA)[2]. These initiatives have created a ripple effect worldwide, compelling organi-
zations not only to secure their infrastructures but to demonstrate rigorous adherence to a
complex, ever-expanding web of legal requirements.
However, this regulatory expansion has introduced significant operational friction. The
variations between frameworks—ranging from the prescriptive controls of ISO 27001[3] to
the risk-based approaches of NIST[4] and the specific mandates of national laws—create a
landscape of immense complexity. For compliance officers and security auditors, the challenge
is no longer just understanding security, but managing the sheer volume of textual analysis
required to map internal policies against these external mandates. The resulting "compliance
fatigue" often leads to manual errors, inconsistencies, and significant financial burdens for
organizations attempting to keep pace with legal changes.
Artificial Intelligence, and specifically the advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs)[5],
has emerged as a transformative force capable of addressing this disparity. While AI is rev-
olutionizing fields from coding to creative writing, its potential in Governance, Risk, and
Compliance (GRC) is particularly profound. By leveraging Natural Language Processing
(NLP) to automate the interpretation and mapping of regulatory texts, AI offers a pathway
to bridge the gap between static policy documents and dynamic legal requirements. This
project explores the practical application of such technologies, aiming to harness the power of
AI to streamline compliance auditing while navigating the technical challenges of accuracy,
privacy, and control.

General Introduction
Report Structure
This report is organized into six chapters, reflecting the iterative "Research-Action"
methodology adopted throughout the project lifecycle:
• Chapter 1: General Framework of the Project establishes the context within
the host organization (CYEN), defines the problem of manual compliance auditing,
and outlines the proposed AI-driven solution.
• Chapter 2: Analysis and Requirements Specification details the functional and
non-functional requirements of the system. It also defines the actors, the Scrum project
management approach, and the collaborative tools used by the team.
• Chapter 3:
Sprints 1 & 2 – Feasibility Study covers the initial exploratory
phase. It documents the testing of Enterprise APIs and the subsequent attempts to
run open-source models on local hardware, highlighting the limitations that drove the
architectural pivot.
• Chapter 4:
Sprint 3 – Cloud Migration and Experimentation details the
deployment of the dedicated Hetzner GPU infrastructure.
It analyzes the "failed"
experiments with advanced reasoning techniques (such as the Critic and Scratchpad
methods) that faced stability issues.
• Chapter 5: Sprint 4 – System Optimization presents the final, stable solution. It
explains the implementation of the "Official Method" using vLLM, the smart_split_policy
algorithm, and the parallel control-centric analysis pipeline.
• Chapter 6:
Sprint 5 – Validation and Hardening provides the quantitative
results of the "20-Policy Benchmark." It also includes a comprehensive security audit
(OWASP Top 10) and the Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), demonstrating the
system’s readiness for production.
The report concludes with a General Conclusion summarizing the project’s contribu-
tions and a discussion on Perspectives and Future Work.

1.6
Proposed Solution: The PoCH Platform
Our graduation project consists of developing PoCH (Policy Checker), a specialized
platform designed to transform the compliance auditing workflow from a manual, error-
prone task into an automated, intelligent process.
Unlike generic AI tools that offer vague summaries, PoCH is engineered to serve as a
precise "Compliance Assistant." It bridges the gap between raw regulatory texts and internal
organizational policies by leveraging state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) within
a strictly controlled, private infrastructure.
Our solution distinguishes itself through a set of key innovations designed to meet the
rigorous demands of cybersecurity consultants:
• Granular "Dissection" Engine: Instead of processing documents as a whole, the
system intelligently slices policies into atomic "rules" (Clauses/Articles). This allows for
deep, line-by-line verification against regulations, eliminating the "needle in a haystack"
problem.
8


===== PAGE 20 =====

Chapter 1. General Framework of the Project
• Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Workflow: We reject the "Black Box" approach. The
solution provides an interactive interface where the Compliance Analyst can review,
edit, or delete the extracted rules before the AI analysis begins, ensuring that the
machine verifies exactly what the human intends.
• Data Sovereignty and Security: To guarantee that sensitive internal policies never
leave the secure perimeter, the entire solution is self-hosted on dedicated GPU infras-
tructure (Hetzner Cloud). No data is sent to public APIs (like OpenAI or Google),
ensuring total compliance with strict NDA and privacy requirements.
• Multi-Framework Agility: The platform is designed to be agnostic. It allows the
user to dynamically select from a library of regulations—including DORA, GDPR,
ISO 27001, NIST, AND Many More !—making it a versatile tool for various
audit scenarios.
• Automated Professional Reporting: To replace hours of manual report writ-
ing, PoCH automatically generates detailed audit reports in .docx and .pdf formats.
These reports include compliance scores, gap analysis, and specific remediation advice
ready for client delivery.
• Control-Centric Analysis: The system utilizes a parallel processing architecture
that checks the entire pool of internal rules against each specific regulatory control,
ensuring a holistic audit that mimics the reasoning of a senior consultant.
• Ergonomic User Interface: Recognizing that compliance officers may not be tech-
nical experts, we designed a simple, web-based interface (No-Code) that abstracts the
complexity of the underlying Python/vLLM backend.
In summary, PoCH aims to act as a force multiplier for the CYEN team, drastically
reducing the time required for gap analysis while maintaining the high standards of accuracy
required in the cybersecurity consulting domain.

Instruction Following (Priority #1)
Why it matters: The stability of the entire system depends on the model’s ability to
return perfectly formatted JSON. If a model adds conversational filler (e.g., "Here is your

• This metric directly tests a model’s ability to adhere to complex formatting constraints
(e.g., "Output exactly 6 keys," "Do not use Markdown"). A high IFEval score is the
single best predictor of JSON reliability.
3.5.2.2
Reasoning & Problem Solving
Why it matters: Compliance auditing is not keyword matching; it requires logical de-
duction. The model must interpret whether a vague policy clause (e.g., "We secure data")
actually satisfies a specific regulatory control (e.g., "Implement AES-256 encryption").

4.8.1
Experiment A: The "Two-Phase" Critic Approach
We hypothesized that a single pass was insufficient for complex analysis. We implemented
a "Critic" workflow (Option A):
1. Phase 1 (Analyst): The model receives the Policy + Regulation and outputs an
analysis.
2. Phase 2 (Critic): The model receives the Policy + Regulation + Phase 1 Out-
put and is asked to "Critique and refine the analysis."
Outcome: Failure. While the quality improved slightly, the "Context Drift" was severe.
Feeding the output back into the input doubled the token usage, causing the model to
exceed the 32k limit rapidly. Furthermore, the latency doubled, making the tool unusable
for real-time work.
4.8.2
Experiment B: The "Scratchpad" (Chain-of-Thought) Method
Inspired by the "ReAct" paper[9], we attempted to let the model "think" before answering.
We instructed the model to output a Thinking Process block before the JSON block.
• The Success: The logical accuracy improved significantly (∼70% correctness). When
the model wrote down its reasoning (e.g., "This clause mentions 8 characters, but the
regulation requires 12..."), the subsequent verdict was usually correct.
• The Failure: JSON Malformatting.
Because the model was generating free-text "thoughts" alongside structured JSON, it
frequently failed to close syntax properly due to token exhaustion or formatting errors. The
parser could not reliably separate the "Thought" from the "JSON," leading to a crash rate
of over 40%.

Enforcing Consistency: The "Guided JSON" Mechanism
The most critical technical breakthrough in this sprint was the elimination of syntax errors.
In Sprint 3, we "asked" the model to output JSON. In Sprint 4, we forced it.
We utilized vLLM’s ‘guided json‘ parameter. Unlike standard prompting, this does not
rely on the model’s "obedience." Instead, it uses Logit Masking[24] based on a Finite State
Machine (FSM).
Technical Implementation: Logit Masking
We defined a raw JSON Schema (not a high-level Pydantic abstraction) to maintain fine-
grained control over the output structure.
1. FSM Construction: Before generation begins, the inference engine converts our
JSON schema into a regex-based Finite State Machine.
2. Token Filtering: At each generation step, the engine evaluates which tokens are valid
according to the schema.
3. Logit Bias: The probability (logit) of any invalid token is set to −∞(negative infin-
ity).

6.5.2.2
LLM02: Insecure Output Handling (XSS)
If an LLM generates a malicious script (e.g., <script>alert(1)</script>), it could
execute in the analyst’s browser.
• Backend Defense: The Guided JSON mechanism acts as a sanitizer at the source.
The Finite State Machine (FSM) enforces a strict schema that does not allow for
free-form HTML tag generation.
• Frontend Defense: All outputs are rendered via textContent or purely as JSON
data, neutralizing potential XSS payloads.
6.5.2.3
LLM04: Model Denial of Service (DoS)
To prevent GPU exhaustion from massive files:
• Nginx Layer: Enforces client_max_body_size 50m.
• Application Layer: A pre-flight token check runs before inference. If len(text)
// 4 exceeds the 16k limit, a 413 Payload Too Large error is returned instantly,
protecting the VRAM.
6.5.2.4
LLM06: Sensitive Information Disclosure
• Stateless Design: The system runs in Inference-Only mode (training=False).
No user data is ever used to fine-tune the model, ensuring that one client’s policy
cannot "leak" into another client’s audit. Data is held in RAM only for the session
duration.

General Conclusion
The PoCH (Policy Checker) project was born from a critical observation within CYEN:
the manual audit of cybersecurity policies is a bottleneck that consumes valuable expertise
on repetitive, low-value tasks. While Generative AI offered a theoretical solution, existing
market tools failed to meet the strict requirements of data sovereignty, accuracy, and granular
analysis required by the EU regulatory landscape.
Through this "Research-Action" initiative, we successfully engineered a sovereign, AI-
driven compliance platform. The project followed a structured evolution detailed throughout
this report:
• Chapter 1 defined the theoretical framework, identifying the privacy risks of com-
mercial APIs and the operational costs of manual auditing.
• Chapter 2 established the requirements and the Agile Scrum methodology, defining
the "Human-in-the-Loop" actors and the secure infrastructure needs.
• Chapter 3 (Sprint 1) served as the feasibility study. It validated the "One-Shot"
reasoning capabilities of LLMs but proved that consumer hardware and local tunneling
were insufficient for professional use.
• Chapter 4 (Sprint 2) marked the pivotal migration to the cloud. We deployed the
Hetzner GPU infrastructure but encountered the limitations of standard prompting
("Scratchpad"), which led to instability.
• Chapter 5 (Sprint 3) achieved system stabilization. By integrating vLLM, imple-
menting the Dissection Algorithm, and utilizing Guided Generation, we created
the "Official Method" that guarantees structured, accurate results.
• Chapter 6 (Sprint 4) validated the solution. Through the "20-Policy Benchmark,"
the system achieved an "A++" rating on the DORA regulation and passed a rigorous
OWASP security audit.

General Conclusion
Project Impact: The PoCH platform has transformed the auditing workflow. Where a

without a single byte of data leaving the controlled European infrastructure. By solving the

Perspectives and Future Work
While the current version of PoCH represents a stable, production-ready tool for individual
audits, the rapid evolution of Generative AI opens new avenues for optimization. To evolve
this project into a full SaaS product and a premier tool for high-level EU compliance auditing,
we identify three key axes for future development.
1. Near Future: Specialized Fine-Tuning
The immediate next step is to transition from a general-purpose instruction-tuned model to
a specialized compliance expert.
• Data-Driven Refinement: We will fine-tune the current Qwen model using the high-
quality "Golden Dataset" generated during our testing phase. This dataset, enriched
with expert corrections for edge cases like multi-line evidence and ambiguous rules, will
directly address the current model’s biases (e.g., over-caution with "Partial/Unclear"
verdicts).
• Enhanced Reliability: This targeted fine-tuning will significantly improve the accu-
racy of the "Analyst" agent, reducing false positives and ensuring remediation advice
is specific and actionable rather than generic.
2. Medium Term: User Experience & Multi-Tenancy
Currently, the system is stateless and session-based. To support professional auditors man-
aging multiple clients:
• Audit History Dashboard: We will implement a lightweight database (PostgreSQL)
to store previous reports. This enables analysts to track "Compliance Drift"—visualizing
how a client’s compliance posture improves or degrades over time (e.g., Q1 vs. Q3
scores).

Perspectives and Future Work
• Multi-Tenancy Security: Introducing a "Client" layer to the data model is essential.
This ensures strict data isolation, guaranteeing that an auditor managing Bank A
cannot accidentally access or cross-reference the policies of Insurance B.
3. Long Term: The "Sovereign Compliance Model"
Our ultimate vision is to move beyond open-source weights to a proprietary, sovereign foun-
dation model.
• Training a Specialist Model: We envision training a dedicated compliance model
from scratch or continuing pre-training on a massive corpus of EU regulations (GDPR,
DORA, AI Act), millions of anonymized corporate policies, and historical compliance
gap scenarios.
• A "Hands-on" Tool for Majors: This specialized model would possess an innate
"legal intuition" that general models lack. It would become an indispensable "hands-on"
tool for major consultancy firms (like Deloitte, KPMG) and EU regulatory bodies, ca-
pable of detecting subtle, systemic compliance gaps that standard tools miss. It would
transition PoCH from a document checker into a "Governance Intelligence Platform"
capable of auditing the entire regulatory posture of multinational organizations.
