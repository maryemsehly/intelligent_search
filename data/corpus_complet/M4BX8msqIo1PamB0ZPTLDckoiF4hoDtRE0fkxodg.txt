Abstract
As part of our final year project, conducted to obtain the national Master’s degree, we collaborated
with Coficab Tunisia to develop an intelligent recruitment platform: RecruTech AI. The project
addresses the fragmentation and inefficiency of traditional recruitment workflows based on emails and
spreadsheets.
We designed a web application using Angular and Flask, integrating NLP, LLMs, and ChartJS.
Our objective was to automate CV analysis, rank candidates intelligently, streamline the recruitment
timeline, and provide real-time dashboards.
The result is a centralized, intelligent, and user-friendly solution that enhances decision-making
and improves the efficiency of HR processes.
Keywords: Angular, Flask, NLP, LLM, ChartJS, Recruitment, Automation

1.3.1 General Context
Coficab Tunisia, like many companies, is currently managing its recruitment process through
external job platforms, such as, LinkedIn and TanitJobs. Offers are published manually, and applications
are collected via direct email or through a Google Form embedded within the LinkedIn post. For
TanitJobs, candidate CVs are retrieved through the employer’s dashboard, downloaded manually, and
then processed one by one.
A single job offer can generate between 150 and 200 applications. Without a centralized recruitment
system, HR professionals are left to manage this influx of candidates through fragmented tools such
as spreadsheets, emails, and cloud storage. In high-volume periods, they often juggle several open
positions simultaneously, leading to delays, loss of information, and inconsistent communication.
1.3.2 Problem Statement
This manual, fragmented approach creates several critical risks and inefficiencies:
• Risk of losing top talent: Promising candidates can slip through the cracks when hundreds of
CVs are processed in spreadsheets or email folders. There is no automated alert for duplicate
applications, nor any way to flag high-potential profiles consistently.
• Inefficient prioritization: Without data-driven scoring, recruiters rely on first-come or alphabetical
order, rather than fit for the role, increasing time to shortlist and introducing bias.
• Lack of end-to-end visibility: Each step—screening, interview scheduling, feedback collection,
final decision—is handled in siloed tools or email threads, making it hard to track a candidate’s
status in real time.
• Time-consuming communications: Handcrafting acceptance or rejection emails for dozens of
candidates per opening is laborious. Delays in response frustrate applicants and degrade employer
brand.
• Scattered feedback: Interviewers share comments via email or informal chat; those notes are
never consolidated, which hampers collaborative decision-making and auditability.

1.4 Basic Concepts

1.4.1 Natural Language Processing (NLP)
Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a field at the intersection of linguistics and artificial intelligence
that focuses on enabling machines to understand, interpret, and generate human language. It plays
a central role in applications such as text classification, summarization, translation, and question
answering. In this project, NLP was used to analyze and interpret unstructured text from CVs and
job descriptions.[2]
1.4.2 Embedding
Embeddings are a way to represent words, sentences, or documents as numerical vectors in a
continuous space.
These representations capture semantic meaning and allow similar texts to be

compared mathematically. Embeddings are widely used in recommendation systems, document clustering,

1.4.3 Large Language Models (LLM)
Large Language Models (LLMs) are deep learning models trained on massive amounts of text data.
They are capable of generating coherent and contextually appropriate responses, completing texts,
summarizing content, and answering questions. These models have become a cornerstone of modern
AI, enabling advanced language understanding and generation.[3]
1.4.4 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that enhances the capabilities of language
models by combining them with external knowledge retrieval. Instead of relying solely on the model’s
internal knowledge, RAG systems first search for relevant information from a database or document
collection, then use this context to generate more accurate and grounded responses.[4]
1.4.5 Linear Support Vector Classification (LinearSVC)
LinearSVC is a machine learning algorithm used for classification tasks. It is based on Support
Vector Machines (SVMs) and is particularly efficient for high-dimensional and sparse datasets, such as
those encountered in text classification. Its simplicity and speed make it a popular choice for building
initial predictive models.[5]
1.4.6 FAISS
FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search) is an open-source library designed to perform fast similarity
searches in large collections of vectors. It is especially useful when dealing with embeddings, enabling
systems to quickly find the most relevant or similar items. FAISS is commonly used in recommendation
engines, search systems, and AI applications requiring high-speed vector comparisons.[6]
1.4.7 Chart.js
Chart.js is a JavaScript library that allows developers to create interactive and visually appealing
charts for web applications. It supports various chart types, such as bar charts, line charts, and pie
charts. In many web dashboards, Chart.js is used to present data in an accessible and user-friendly
manner, helping users to interpret and analyze metrics quickly.[7]

Agile is a project management philosophy that promotes iterative development, close stakeholder

3.3.3 AI API Implementation
To meet the functional needs identified in the sprint use case diagram, specifically the user stories
"View Candidates", "View CV Summary", and "Add Offer", we integrated AI-driven services into
the application. These APIs enhance candidate search, automate CV summarization, and improve
job offer quality. The integration combines semantic search with vector similarity and large language
models to provide advanced filtering, ranking, and text generation capabilities.
3.3.3.1 NLP-Based Intelligent CV Search
To enhance candidate retrieval and enable semantic search over CV content, we developed a
sophisticated NLP pipeline. This system allows Admin and HR users to perform advanced searches
on submitted resumes, yielding more relevant results based on both exact matches and contextual
similarity.
Figure 3.13 presents the full NLP pipeline involved in this functionality. It illustrates the end-to-end
flow, from PDF parsing to semantic ranking using vector similarity and FAISS indexing.
Figure 3.13: NLP pipeline for intelligent CV search
The feature enables users to enter free-form queries (e.g., “full-stack Python developer with Docker
experience”), which are then processed and matched against the content of CVs using a similarity-based
scoring system. Candidates with higher relevance scores are returned at the top of the result list.
52


===== PAGE 69 =====

Chapter 3. Release One – Implementation of Core Functionalities
The pipeline involves the following steps:
• PDF Text Extraction: Extracts raw content from CV files.
• Text Preprocessing: Cleans and tokenizes the text.
• Embedding Generation and Storage: Computes a vector using ‘all-mpnet-base-v2‘ and stores it.
• Query Preprocessing: Applies the same preprocessing to the search query.
• Similarity Scoring: Uses cosine similarity between embeddings.
• FAISS Integration: Enables fast approximate nearest-neighbor search.
A literal match boost is also applied to ensure exact keyword hits get higher weight. By caching
embeddings in the database, we avoid redundant computations, improving performance.
This system brings significant value to the recruitment process by enabling context-aware, intelligent
CV filtering aligned with semantic intent.
3.3.3.2 Integration of Large Language Models (LLMs)
To enhance the intelligence and usability of the system, we integrated Large Language Models
(LLMs) into two core functionalities:
CV summarization and job description correction.
These
capabilities are powered by the llama3-70b-8192 model, a high-performance generative language
model accessed through the Groq API. Figure 3.14 illustrates the pipeline used for integrating LLMs
into the recruitment platform.
Figure 3.14: Pipeline of LLM integration for CV summarization and job description correction
Groq API Integration
Groq provides high-speed, low-latency access to advanced LLMs via a
scalable and developer-friendly API. This allowed us to integrate cutting-edge language processing
without managing model infrastructure.
The API supports structured input, making it ideal for
real-time summarization and rewriting tasks within the recruitment workflow.
Model Used
The model employed is llama3-70b-8192, a 70-billion-parameter transformer optimized
for complex language understanding and generation.
It supports both English and French and is
well-suited for summarization, grammar correction, and tone improvement.

• CV Summarization: After text is extracted from a candidate’s CV, it is sent to the model
to produce a short, readable summary.
This helps HR users quickly assess the candidate’s
experience, skills, and educational background without reading the entire document.
• Job Description Correction: When HR or Admin users create or edit job offers, they can request

4.2.3 RAG-Based Intelligent Candidate Ranking
To implement the user story "View AI Recommendations" defined in the sprint use case diagram,
we developed an advanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline to enhance the accuracy
and intelligence of candidate ranking. This approach combines semantic similarity search, job-specific
boosting, and reranking via a large language model to produce a final list of candidates aligned with
the job requirements.
Figure 4.4 illustrates the overall architecture of the RAG pipeline, from initial filtering and FAISS
indexing to LLM-powered reranking.
Figure 4.4: RAG pipeline for intelligent candidate ranking
Before initiating the semantic ranking, the system performs basic filtering based on structured job
constraints:
• Minimum experience,
• Required age,
• Level of study,
• Job category,
• Field of study.
Only candidates who meet these filters are passed to the RAG ranking pipeline, which operates in
the following stages:
• Embedding Verification and Generation: For each candidate, the system checks if a precomputed
embedding exists. If not, it generates one using the ‘all-mpnet-base-v2‘ model and stores it in
the database.
82


===== PAGE 99 =====

• FAISS-Based Similarity Scoring: The job description is converted into an embedding and
compared with candidate embeddings using cosine similarity. A FAISS index is created with:
index = faiss.IndexFlatIP(768)
• Boosting:
– Add +0.15 for each required skill found in the CV.
– Add +0.25 for each required certification.
These are added to the base FAISS similarity to compute the total score.
• Cosine Similarity Calculation:
cosine_similarity(A, B) =
A · B
∥A∥× ∥B∥
(4.1)
• Top-N Candidate Selection: The candidates with the top N boosted scores are passed to the
LLM for reranking.
• LLM-Based Reranking: Using the ‘llama3-70b-8192‘ model via the Groq API, the job description
and CV summaries are provided as structured prompts. The LLM returns a reranked list of the
top candidates based on relevance and context.
• Final Output: The result includes:
– Candidate ID and Name,
– CV Summary,
– Age, Education, and Experience,
– FAISS Score, Boosted Score,
– Final Rank from the LLM.
This AI-powered pipeline provides a scalable and intelligent solution for candidate ranking, ensuring
fair, fast, and accurate results aligned with recruiter expectations.

4.3.3.1 Why we chose Chart.js for the project
Chart.js provides a highly performant and versatile solution that seamlessly integrates with Angular,
enabling the creation of sophisticated, responsive, and interactive charts directly within the frontend
framework.
One of the key advantages of Chart.js is its native compatibility with Angular, allowing us to
leverage its powerful features without requiring additional external libraries or complex workarounds.
This results in a streamlined development process and improved maintainability. Moreover, Chart.js
delivers rapid rendering and smooth animations, ensuring a fluid user experience even when visualizing
large and dynamic datasets.

By combining Chart.js on the frontend with Flask-based REST APIs on the backend, our platform

and rich interactivity, empowering users to explore recruitment statistics through intuitive charts and
filters without performance compromise or integration overhead.
4.3.3.2 Overview of the Dashboard
The dashboard presents a comprehensive visual summary of key recruitment metrics, designed to
support HR decision-making with clear and actionable insights. The following charts are included in
the dashboard:
• Candidates Status Charts: Visualize the distribution of rejected, hired, selected, and total
candidates, offering a high-level overview of the recruitment pipeline.
• Candidates with Zero Hires: Identifies job offers that have received applications but resulted
in zero hires, helping HR teams pinpoint roles that may require attention or adjustment.
• Job Categories per Offer: Displays the types of job categories candidates belong to for each
job offer, providing insight into the reach and attractiveness of posted positions.
• Interviews per Month: Shows the number of interviews conducted monthly within a selected
year, allowing analysis of recruitment effort and timing.
• Interviews per User: Highlights the total number of interviews handled by each HR user,
supporting workload distribution analysis.
• Fields of Study Distribution: Represents the academic disciplines of candidates across all
applications, as well as broken down per offer, enabling diversity and specialization tracking.
• Accepted Candidates Trend Over Years: Tracks the yearly evolution of accepted candidates,
helping to monitor the effectiveness of hiring campaigns over time.
4.3.3.3 Filtering Capabilities
To allow for more targeted analysis, the dashboard supports the following filters across all charts:
• Year: Focus on statistics from a specific year.
• Job Category: Analyze metrics related to a selected role.
• Field of Study: Visualize data concerning candidates with a given academic background.
The combination of these interactive visualizations and filtering options ensures that the dashboard
delivers valuable, real-time insights that help optimize the recruitment process.

General Conclusion
The RecruTech AI project marked a major digital transformation in Coficab Tunisia’s recruitment
process, evolving from a manual and fragmented workflow into a centralized, intelligent platform
powered by cutting-edge technologies such as Natural Language Processing (NLP), Machine Learning
(ML) and Large Language Models (LLMs).
The development was structured into two major releases.
Release One, comprising Sprints 1
to 3, focused on delivering the core functionalities: user authentication, role-based access, job offer
and application management, and customizable recruitment workflows. These foundational elements
ensured the system’s usability, scalability, and alignment with HR processes.
Release Two, covering Sprints 4 and 5, brought advanced features that significantly elevated the
platform’s intelligence and interactivity. Sprint 4 introduced a detailed hiring process overview and
a RAG-based candidate recommendation system. Sprint 5 enhanced the user experience by adding a
real-time notification system and an integrated dashboard for visualizing key performance indicators.
By automating repetitive tasks, enabling AI-assisted decision-making, and centralizing all recruitment
operations in a unified interface, RecruTech AI has considerably improved efficiency, transparency, and
collaboration for HR and administrative users. The platform not only addresses current operational
challenges but also establishes a robust and extensible foundation for future innovations in recruitment
at Coficab Tunisia.
