General Introduction
In today’s fast-paced world, maintaining fitness and a healthy lifestyle has become more crucial
than ever. Whether you’re a professional athlete striving for peak performance or a fitness enthu-
siast seeking expert guidance, personalized coaching can be the key to success. However, finding
the right coach or nutritionist—and sticking to a tailored plan with measurable progress—remains
a challenge for many.
During my internship at DigiSubs Agency, I had the opportunity to contribute to the de-
velopment of TRAINOVO, an innovative mobile app designed to bridge this gap. TRAINOVO
connects users with certified fitness coaches and nutritionists, offering personalized workout plans,
science-backed nutrition guidance, and real-time progress tracking. By making professional coach-
ing accessible, structured, and results-driven, the app empowers users to achieve their fitness goals
efficiently—all from their smartphones.
This report documents my work on the TRAINOVO project. Chapter One outlines the back-
ground and development approach, while Chapter Two details the functional and non-functional
requirements, system architecture, and development environment. The remaining three chapters
delve into the sprint backlog, design iterations, and technical implementation, providing a com-
plete overview of the app’s lifecycle.
Through this report, I demonstrate how TRAINOVO simplifies the fitness journey by com-
bining expert knowledge, data-driven insights, and an intuitive coaching experience. Whether

1.5.4
The Scrum Process Flow
The Scrum Cycle consists of repetitive steps designed to deliver a product increment at the end
of each sprint. The cycle follows this general flow:
• Product Backlog: The first step in the cycle is determining and prioritizing the product
backlog. This is a list of new features, modifications, or fixes that require development. The
Product Owner creates and maintains this backlog.
• Sprint Planning: At the beginning of every sprint, the Scrum Team conducts a planning
meeting where, collectively, they choose product backlog items that they will work on during
the coming sprint. They establish an objective for the sprint and define the scope of the
task at hand.
• Sprint: The sprint is a time-boxed iteration where the Development Team works to com-
plete the selected backlog items. It typically lasts 2 to 4 weeks. The team holds daily
stand-up meetings to stay on track.
• Sprint Review: The Scrum Team will hold the sprint review at the end of the sprint,
encompassing a demonstration of completed work.
Stakeholders will provide feedback,
which might require some adjustments to the product backlog.
• Sprint Retrospective: After the Sprint Review, the team holds a retrospective meeting
to reflect on the sprint’s process. The goal is to identify areas for improvement in teamwork,
tools, and processes.
• Repeat: The cycle repeats with the next sprint, starting again with Sprint Planning. The
Scrum Team continues iterating on the product, gradually increasing its functionality and
quality with each sprint.

Software Architecture
MVC is a design pattern that separates an application into three interconnected components: the
Model, the View, and the Controller. This separation improves maintainability, testability, and
scalability by isolating business logic, user interface, and data handling.
• Model : The Model represents the application’s data and core business logic. It handles
data retrieval from sources (databases, APIs, etc.), enforces validation rules, and manages
storage operations. The Model operates independently of how data is displayed or user
interactions are processed. In some implementations, it can notify other components of data
changes using patterns like Observer, ensuring synchronization without direct dependencies.
• View : The View focuses solely on presenting information to the user and capturing their
input. It receives data (either directly from the Model or via the Controller) and renders it in
the appropriate format (UI elements, pages, etc.). The View contains no business logic—its
role is purely visual. When users interact with the interface (e.g., clicking a button), the
View forwards these actions to the Controller for processing.

• Controller : The Controller acts as the intermediary between the Model and View. It
processes user input received from the View, triggers relevant operations in the Model
(e.g., updating or fetching data), and determines how the View should reflect changes.
Unlike passive data-binding approaches, the Controller explicitly manages updates, making
it responsible for flow control and ensuring the Model and View remain decoupled.
