General Introduction 
In the last few decades, the video game industry has evolved from a niche hobby into a global 
cultural and economic powerhouse. What once began as simple 2D arcade experiences has 
now transformed into immersive, cinematic, and technically sophisticated interactive worlds 
enjoyed by millions.  
According to recent market report made by mediacat.uk, the global gaming industry 
surpassed $180 billion in annual revenue, compared to around $100 billion for film and $30 
billion for music. This growth is not only driven by blockbuster titles and massive studios but 
also by independent developers who continue to innovate, push creative boundaries, and 
serve emerging niches. 
This report explores the development of Tape 101, an independent psychological horror game 
created using Unreal Engine. The game is a blend of immersive storytelling, eerie liminal 
environments, and experimental gameplay mechanics, made possible through the tools and 
workflows that define modern indie game development. 
This thesis summarizes all the work we have accomplished in this context. Apart from the 
introduction and conclusion, it is structured into five chapters: 
Chapter 1: General Context – introduces the Steam platform, the chosen horror genre, the 
conceptual foundation of liminal spaces, a study and critique of existing games, and the final 
proposed game idea: Tape 101. 
Chapter 2: Game Design – outlines the technical tools used in the production of the game. 
Chapter 3: Technologies Used, UML, & Game Mechanics – presents the concept of the 
game design document, core gameplay mechanics, UML diagrams, and unique features 
integrated into the player experience. 
Chapter 4: Aesthetic Design – focuses on the visual and environmental design choices, 
including shaders, level design, and artistic direction to support the horror atmosphere. 
Chapter 5: Technical Specifications – covers the development hardware, optimization 
methods, blueprint efficiency, scalability, performance settings, and user configuration 
options.

1.3-The Fictional Concept of The Backrooms 
In 2022 a YouTube video uploaded by the indie film maker "Kane Pixel" has gone viral 
reaching over 60 million views. 
This video sparked global interest in the concept of The Backrooms—a surreal, endless maze 
of emptiness, decaying rooms embodying the eerie feeling of liminal spaces. These settings 
evoke nostalgia, unease, and existential dread, making them a perfect backdrop for 
psychological horror narratives. 
 
Figure 1.6: Kane Pixel’s Viral Concept

1.4.1-Study of Existing Games 
Following the viral rise of the Backrooms and liminal space aesthetics, many indie 
developers adapted the concept into playable experiences. Notable titles such as Escape the 
Backrooms, Inside the Backrooms, and various Steam projects exploring the eerie and surreal 
environments inspired by these internet-born myths.

1.4.2-Critique of Existing Games 
While these games initially gained attention, they often fall short in execution. Common 
criticisms include poor level design, an overreliance on cheap jump scares, and a lack of any 
meaningful narrative. Most projects focus solely on the horror layer, neglecting the 
psychological dimension that defines liminal spaces. As a result, players are left with shallow 
experiences that feel disconnected from the emotional depth these environments could offer. 
 
1.4.3-Proposed Solution: Tape 101 
Tape 101 addresses these gaps by introducing a unique mechanical hook—a remote control 
car used for solving environmental puzzles—and placing strong emphasis on psychological 
horror rather than superficial scares. The game weaves a narrative thread throughout its 
levels. 
By blending gameplay innovation with environmental storytelling, Tape 101 aims to deliver a 
richer and more unsettling interpretation of the liminal space mythos.

1.5-Project Methodology 
For the development of Tape 101, I adopted the Agile SCRUM methodology, tailoring its 
principles to fit a solo development context. I assumed all SCRUM roles: 
• 
As the Product Owner, I defined the vision and organized all features and tasks in 
the Product Backlog. 
• 
As the SCRUM Master, I ensured a smooth and focused workflow, removing any 
personal obstacles. 
• 
As the SCRUM Team, I executed all tasks across programming, design, testing, and 
more. 
 
 
Figure 1.8: Scrum Process 
 
I planned the development in sprints, using Sprint Planning sessions to select and define 
tasks for each Sprint Backlog. While there was no formal team meeting, I adapted the Daily 
SCRUM by keeping mental notes or simple entries in a notepad to track daily progress and 
challenges. Similarly, I conducted Sprint Reviews and Retrospectives alone — reviewing 
what was completed, what could be improved, and updating the backlog accordingly. This 
approach allowed me to stay agile, organized, and responsive even as a solo developer.

2.1.1-What is GDD? 
A Game Design Document is a comprehensive blueprint that outlines the vision, gameplay 
systems, mechanics, Visual style, and narrative structure of a game. It acts as a central 
reference for developers, designers, and collaborators throughout production. A well-
structured GDD ensures cohesion, reduces ambiguity, and aligns creative and technical goals. 
Its key components include core gameplay mechanics, story flow, level structure, user 
interface, and technical constraints.

2.1.2-GDD Visual Style 
We have adopted the VHS found footage aesthetic to reinforce the analogue horror identity of 
Tape 101. This visual direction fits naturally with the liminal/backrooms concept, evoking 
nostalgia and unease through low-fidelity imagery, scan lines, and degraded colours. The 
VHS filter adds a layer of low-fidelity realism, making the experience feel more immersive 
and unsettling. 
 
2.1.3-GDD Gameplay Features 
Focus on slow paced psychological exploration within unsettling, liminal environments. 
2.1.3.1-Integration of the RC Car Mechanic Used to: 
 Explore inaccessible areas. 
 Solve specific environmental or visibility-based puzzles. 
2.1.3.2-Emphasis on Interaction with Object to: 
 Progress through the level. 
 Uncover subtle narrative elements 
2.1.3.3-Designed to: 
 Create tension & immersion through the environment and audio design. 
 Focus on the psycho-horror elements without relying on jump scares. 
2.1.4-GDD Narrative 
2.1.4.1-Story 
The game follows Dr. Alan (The player), a researcher and a rescuer who is sent into the 
Backrooms via a secret portal developed by the MPRF (Multidimensional Paranormal 
Research Facility). His mission: to track any traces or evidence on the disappearance of Dr. 
John, one of the first researchers who mysteriously vanished inside the realm. The entire 
experience is presented as a found footage tape retrieved by MPRF after Alan himself goes 
missing.

2.1.4.2-Narrative Hook 
Over time, Alan discovers he is caught in a time loop, reliving parts of his failed rescue 
attempt as reality collapses. The story unfolds slowly through player exploration, 
environmental storytelling, and recovered logs. 
2.1.4.3-Cinematic 
Key moments in the game are portrayed through in-engine cinematic sequences, reinforcing 
tension, atmosphere, and narrative beats without removing player immersion. 
2.1.4.4-Text Speech & Dialogue System 
Dialogue between Alan and the MPRF command is delivered through a minimalist text-based 
interface, emulating radio transmissions and logs to maintain the analogue horror tone.

3.3-3D Modelling 
3D modelling is a crucial phase in game development, responsible for creating all the in-
game visual assets and environments. For Tape 101, two main tools were used: Unreal 
Engine’s built-in modelling tools for quick edits and prototyping, and Blender for creating 
detailed and optimized assets. 
3.3.1-UE5 Modelling Tool 
Unreal Engine 5’s native modelling tools were mainly used for creating simple and modular 
architectural geometry, such as walls, floors, ceilings, and frames. Since Tape 101 relies 
heavily on indoor liminal environments with repetitive patterns, there was no need for 
complex modelling in most cases. UE5's modelling capabilities provided a fast, optimized 
workflow to block out and design levels directly inside the engine without switching between 
external tools.

3.3.2-Blender 
Blender is an open source free but a very powerful 3D modelling software, it was utilized for 
more complex models and physics-based assets that required high visual fidelity and realism. 
It was particularly useful in crafting PBR (Physically Based Rendering) meshes, such as 
detailed props, interactable objects, and environmental clutter. These assets added depth to 
the world of Tape 101, enhancing immersion while aligning with the game’s analogue horror 
atmosphere. Once completed, the models were textured and exported into UE5 for in-game 
integration.

5.2.1.3-LOD 
LOD (Level of Detail) is a performance optimization technique that automatically swaps 3D 
models with lower-resolution versions as the distance between the camera and the object 
increases. This reduces the rendering cost while keeping visual fidelity high up close. 
For example, an object has 3000 polygons in a standard camera view distance, if you make 
the object far away from the camera or invisible there is absolutely no need to make the GPU 
calculate all the 3000 polygons, so LODs comes handy to reduce those polygons to half of 
the number or less, increasing performance.

5.2.2.3-Optimizing Demanding Features 
One of the most CPU-intensive gameplay systems in Tape 101 is the interact feature shared 
between the Player and the RC car. This system relies on a constant line trace every second to 
detect interactable objects in the world, which can become expensive when left always 
running. 
To optimize this, a Boolean variable is used to activate the line trace only when needed. The 
Boolean is set to true when the Player or RC overlaps a Trigger Box near interactable zones. 
Once they leave the area, the Boolean is reset to false, effectively disabling the line trace and 
reducing unnecessary CPU usage.

General Conclusion 
Tape 101 is a distinctive psychological horror game that brings innovation to the indie game 
development scene through its integration of liminal space aesthetics, environmental 
storytelling, and a unique gameplay mechanic centred around a remote-controlled car. Built 
using Unreal Engine 5, the project demonstrates a balance between creativity, technical 
execution, and genre relevance. By focusing on atmosphere, immersive exploration, and 
narrative depth, Tape 101 elevates the backrooms mythos beyond surface-level horror. 
Throughout this thesis project, we have explored the full development cycle—from 
conceptualization and design documentation to engine use, modelling, texturing, and 
optimization. Each phase provided practical experience with professional tools such as UE5, 
Blender, and Substance Painter, while also emphasizing performance scalability and good 
development practices. 
This work has been a valuable opportunity to sharpen both technical and project management 
skills in a real-world game production context. Looking forward, future development may 
expand the game with additional levels, richer NPC interactions, and deeper lore integration. 
The goal is to release Tape 101 on Steam, with the ambition of gaining recognition in the 
indie horror space and inspiring other developers to explore emotionally rich and 
mechanically meaningful experiences.
