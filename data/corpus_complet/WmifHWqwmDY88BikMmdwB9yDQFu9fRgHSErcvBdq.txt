INTRODUCTION
Saidi IRAD
Introduction
Football has long been considered not only the most popular sport in the world but also a domain
rich with complex strategies, tactical choices, and rapid decision-making. In recent years, the
integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Computer Vision (CV) into sports analytics has
opened new horizons for the understanding and optimization of game performance. Clubs,
analysts, and broadcasters are increasingly turning to intelligent systems capable of automating
video analysis, tracking players and the ball, and providing advanced tactical insights. Despite
these developments, current solutions remain limited in terms of accessibility, explainability,
and real-time deployment.
This thesis fits into this context and aims to design and implement an AI-driven football
tactical analysis system, capable of processing match videos in real time. The proposed system
combines deep learning models for detection and tracking, advanced optimization techniques
for real-time inference, and a user-friendly dashboard application for visualization and interac-
tion. The work seeks to bridge the gap between cutting-edge research in AI and its practical
application to football analytics.
The problem addressed in this thesis is twofold: first, the reliable detection and tracking
of objects of interest on the pitch (players, referees, and the ball), despite challenges such as
occlusions, fast movements, and small object sizes; second, the integration of these models into
a robust and accessible pipeline that can deliver insights to end-users in real time. To tackle

these challenges, the project adopts a CRISP-ML(Q) methodology, ensuring a structured and
iterative approach to data understanding, model design, evaluation, and deployment.
The main contributions of this thesis can be summarized as follows:
• Advanced Multi-Object Detection and Tracking Pipeline: The design and implemen-
tation of a comprehensive detection and tracking system using YOLOv11 for object detec-
tion and OCSort for multi-object tracking with re-identification capabilities, specifically
adapted for football analysis challenges.
• Real-Time Tactical Analysis Engine: The development of tactical analysis algorithms
that operate in real-time, including team formation detection, player marking analysis,
spatial heatmap generation, and possession statistics calculation.
• Performance Optimization and Real-Time Processing: The integration of optimiza-
tion techniques including CUDA acceleration, TensorRT model optimization, and FP16
precision to ensure efficient real-time deployment with 30+ FPS processing speed.
• Comprehensive Dashboard Application: The development of a cross-platform dash-
board application built with Flutter and Firebase integration, providing intuitive visualiza-
tion of processed data and enabling real-time user interaction.
• Experimental Validation and Performance Evaluation: The comprehensive experi-
mental evaluation of the proposed system using public datasets and real match videos,
with performance comparisons against existing solutions demonstrating superior accu-
racy while maintaining real-time processing capabilities.
• Accessible and Scalable Architecture: The creation of an open-source, accessible sys-
tem that operates on standard broadcast footage without requiring expensive hardware,
making it suitable for professional clubs, academies, and researchers.
This thesis is organized into five chapters:

• Chapter 1: Introduction and Context introduces the general problem, defines the ob-

demonstration of the results.
In summary, this thesis aims to contribute to the growing field of AI-driven sports analytics
by proposing a complete and deployable solution for football tactical analysis. Through the
combination of advanced models, real-time optimizations, and a user-oriented interface, the
project seeks not only to demonstrate the feasibility of automated tactical analysis but also to
lay the groundwork for future enhancements in multi-camera integration, tactical explainability,
and advanced decision support.

1.1 Introduction
This chapter establishes the foundational framework for developing an AI-driven football tacti-
cal analysis system. The chapter begins by introducing QuetraTech, the host organization that
provided the professional context for this research project.
The chapter then examines the current state of the art in football analytics, analyzing existing
commercial solutions including video-based tactical analysis platforms, wearable technologies,
and integrated data systems, while critically evaluating their limitations in terms of accessibility,
cost, and real-time capabilities.
Following this analysis, the chapter defines the specific research problem: the need for an
accessible, real-time football analysis system that operates without expensive hardware while
providing advanced tactical insights. The proposed solution is then outlined, emphasizing its
key innovations in multi-object detection, tactical analysis, and user interface design.
Finally, the chapter presents the methodological framework based on an adapted CRISP-
ML(Q) approach, ensuring a structured development process from business understanding
through deployment and continuous improvement.
The chapter serves as a comprehensive introduction to the project’s context, objectives, and
methodological approach, setting the stage for the detailed technical discussions that follow.

Assistant Referee (VAR) and wearable GPS trackers, which provide data on player movements

1.5 Existing Solutions
In recent years, numerous companies and research institutions have developed innovative solu-
tions that leverage Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies for football analysis. These systems
typically concentrate on three primary domains: video-based tactical analysis, wearable tech-
nologies, and integrated data platforms.

Leading companies in this domain include Metrica Sports, Hudl, and StatsBomb, which of-

detection and tracking of players, referees, and the ball, while generating detailed statistics
including possession metrics, passing networks, and spatial occupation patterns. Advanced
systems such as Second Spectrum and SkillCorner incorporate deep learning methodologies
to deliver real-time tactical visualizations and predictive models for player movement pat-

detection combined with OCSort tracking algorithms to detect and track players, referees, and
the ball in real time, providing the foundational data for all subsequent analysis. The second
model utilizes YOLOv11 specifically trained for field keypoint detection to identify and track
critical field reference points, enabling accurate mapping between video coordinates and real-
world pitch dimensions.
The tactical analysis component computes team formations, and spatial control based on
detected positions and pitch mapping.

Justification for CRISP-ML(Q) Selection
The football computer vision project involves multiple advanced AI components such as real-
time detection, tracking, and spatial transformation of objects on the field. This requires not
only high model accuracy but also reproducibility, reliability, and trust in the results.
Among the three methodologies, CRISP-ML(Q) stands out as the most suitable for this
type of project. It combines the structured approach of CRISP-DM (ensuring clear objectives,
clean data, and proper evaluation) with the automation and scalability of MLOps. Furthermore,
it introduces a robust Quality Assurance (Q) layer that enforces model traceability, fairness,
bias control, explainability, and continuous validation.
The specific advantages of CRISP-ML(Q) for this football analytics project include:
• Comprehensive Quality Assurance: The built-in quality gates ensure that both detection
and tracking models meet accuracy standards before deployment, crucial for real-time
football analysis where errors can significantly impact tactical insights.
• Real-time System Optimization: The methodology’s emphasis on continuous monitor-
ing and validation is essential for maintaining performance in live match scenarios where
system reliability is paramount.
• Research and Production Balance: Unlike CRISP-DM which focuses on research or
MLOps which emphasizes production, CRISP-ML(Q) supports both academic research
objectives and practical deployment requirements, making it ideal for a thesis project with
real-world applications.
• Computer Vision Specificity: The methodology is specifically optimized for modern
machine learning and computer vision workflows, providing structured guidance for the
complex pipeline of detection, tracking, and tactical analysis.
• Ethical and Explainable AI: The mandatory governance requirements ensure that the
system’s decisions can be explained and validated, which is crucial for football analysis
where coaches and analysts need to understand and trust the system’s recommendations.

2.6 Data Characteristics and Challenges
Football match data presents several significant challenges for computer vision models that
require sophisticated solutions to ensure accurate and reliable analysis:
• Ball Detection and Tracking Challenges: The football is extremely small relative to the
field size, moves at high velocities, and can be visually similar to background elements
or players’ socks, making consistent detection and tracking particularly difficult.

dataset is then validated, split, and versioned for reproducible experiments.
