General Introduction
In today’s rapidly evolving digital landscape, advanced technologies like machine learning
and computer vision are transforming industries, making them more efficient, accurate,
and scalable. The sports media industry is no exception. With the growing demand for
real-time sports content, particularly football highlights, content creation workflows have
faced significant pressure to meet high expectations for speed and quality.
Traditionally, the process of generating football match highlights has been manual,
requiring editors to sift through hours of footage to identify key moments. This process
is not only time-consuming but also prone to human bias and subjectivity, leading to
inconsistencies in the final content.
Additionally, with the increasing demand for on-
demand and live sports highlights, traditional methods have become insufficient.
This project proposes an automated football highlight generation system based on
deep learning and computer vision.
By leveraging advanced object detection models
such as YOLOv10 and YOLOv8, this system is capable of analyzing full-match videos
and automatically extracting the most relevant moments, including goals, fouls, and key
plays. The system also utilizes a spatial understanding of the pitch for accurate detection
of critical events like shots inside the penalty area.
The proposed solution aims to significantly speed up the highlight production process,
ensuring consistency and scalability, while reducing the reliance on manual intervention.
This system will be integrated into a user-friendly web interface, allowing users to upload
match videos and receive automatically generated highlights in a seamless and efficient
manner.
This dissertation is organized as follows:
• Chapter 1 - General Context: Introduces the project, the host company (KEY’S
Agency), the problem statement, and the proposed solution, followed by the method-
ology used.
• Chapter 2 - Technical Background and Domain Understanding: Discusses the key
technologies behind the project, including computer vision, machine learning, and
deep learning, with a focus on YOLO models.

1.1.3
Problem Statement
In the current football content production workflow, the process of generating match
highlights is largely manual and labor-intensive.
Editors must review the full-length
match footage, identify significant moments such as goals, fouls, and key plays, and then
extract and assemble these into a coherent highlight reel. This process is time-consuming,
subject to human bias, and often lacks consistency in what is deemed "highlight-worthy."
Furthermore, as demand for real-time sports content increases, especially on digital
platforms, traditional manual methods struggle to meet the need for rapid turnaround.
The delay in delivering highlights can reduce viewer engagement, limit monetization op-
portunities, and create a competitive disadvantage in media and broadcasting.
These challenges highlight the growing need for an intelligent, automated solution that
can accurately and efficiently generate football highlights, ensuring consistency, speed, and
scalability in modern sports media production.

1.1.4
Proposed Solution
To automate the process of generating football match highlights, we propose developing
a smart video analysis system that processes full-length match videos and extracts the
most relevant moments based on ball position and player presence.
The highlight generation is done in two main stages, each using a distinct model
and logic.
The first step is to process the original match video at a rate of 1 frame per
second. For each frame, the system checks whether there are at least five players
detected and whether the football pitch is visible. Only the seconds (frames) that
satisfy both conditions are retained. These selected frames are then assembled into a
temporary intermediate video, which significantly reduces irrelevant portions of the match
and narrows the analysis to meaningful game moments.
In the second step, the intermediate video is further analyzed to detect events where
the ball is inside the penalty area. For each detected event, the system automatically
extracts a short clip that includes 5 seconds before and 5 seconds after the event.
This ensures enough context is preserved around each key moment. These short clips are
then concatenated chronologically to form the final highlight video.
This pipeline is powered by two specialized object detection models:
• A YOLOv10-based model to detect players, referees, goalkeepers, and the ball.
• A YOLOv8-based model to detect the pitch and its 32 keypoints, enabling spatial
awareness for penalty zone detection.
The final result is a condensed, context-rich highlight video that captures important
moments of the match without requiring manual intervention.
The entire process is accessible through a user-friendly interface, where users can
upload a full match video and download the automatically generated highlight clip directly
from the web application. This makes the system fast, efficient, and practical for a wide
range of users.

Knowledge Discovery in Databases (KDD)

consists of five sequential phases:
1. Data Selection: Identification of relevant data sources and target subsets.

3. Data Transformation: Feature engineering and dimensionality reduction (nor-

ing, association analysis).
5. Pattern Evaluation: Validation of discovered patterns for statistical significance
and business relevance.
This structured approach ensures the transformation of low-level data into high-value
knowledge while maintaining methodological rigor. The iterative nature allows refinement
at each stage based on domain feedback and validation metrics.

Supervised learning builds upon several foundational concepts that are critical for training

Dataset
A dataset [20] refers to a structured collection of data used for training and evaluating

function is essential for improving model accuracy and overall performance.

Performance Metrics
Performance metrics[29] are crucial for evaluating the effectiveness of a machine learning
model. These metrics help evaluate the model’s performance on different tasks, especially
in distinguishing classes in classification problems.
Precision: Precision[30] is the ratio of correctly predicted positive observations to
the total predicted positive observations. It measures the accuracy of positive predictions,
making it important in situations where false positives are costly.
Recall: Recall[31] is the ratio of correctly predicted positive observations to all actual
positives. This indicates how well the model can identify all positive instances in the data,
which is crucial in scenarios where the absence of positive cases is undesirable.
F1 Score: The F1 score[32] is the harmonic mean of precision and recall. It provides
a balanced measure of model performance, especially when there is an uneven distribution
of classes or when precision and recall are important.

Challenges in Model Training
Training machine learning models often involves challenges such as underfitting and over-
fitting, which can significantly impact model performance.
2.4.6.1
Underfitting
Underfitting[33] occurs when the model is too simple to capture the underlying patterns
in the data, resulting in poor performance on the training and testing sets. This usually
happens when the model does not have the complexity to learn from the data.
2.4.6.2
Overfitting
Overfitting[34] occurs when the model becomes too complex, learning not only the un-
derlying patterns but also the noise present in the training data. As a result, the model
performs well on the training set but poorly on the new unseen data.
Figure 2.9: Model Fitting Scenarios
2.4.6.3
Improvement Techniques
To avoid both underfitting and overfitting, several improvement techniques can be applied
during the model training phase:

Data Augmentation:
Data augmentation[35] is a technique used to increase the size of
the training dataset by generating altered versions of the existing data. These alterations
can include operations such as flipping, rotating, or changing the color of images. This
technique helps the model become more adaptable by exposing it to a wider range of
variations, thereby enhancing its ability to generalize to new, unseen data.
Regularization:
Regularization[36] techniques such as L1 and L2 regularization in-
troduce a penalty to the model’s cost function to discourage overfitting. By penalizing
overly complex models, regularization encourages simpler models that perform better on
new data. This technique is essential for preventing the model from memorizing the noise
in the training set.
Dropout:
Dropout[37] is a regularization method in which a random subset of neurons
are "dropped" or ignored during training. This prevents the model from becoming overly
reliant on specific neurons, thus reducing the likelihood of overfitting. Dropout encourages
the network to learn more robust features that are not dependent on any particular set
of neurons.
Early Stopping:
Early stopping[38] is a technique used to halt training once the
model’s performance on the validation set stops improving after a certain number of
iterations. This method prevents overfitting by stopping training before the model begins
to memorize the noise in the training data. Early stopping is often used in conjunction
with dropout to help balance the trade-off between underfitting and overfitting.

The application workflow outlines how the uploaded football match video is analyzed and
transformed into a condensed highlight video using a multi-stage processing pipeline. The
steps are as follows:
Video Upload Input: The user initiates the process by uploading a full-length football
match video through the Next.js-based web interface. The video is then transferred to
the backend for processing.
Initial Frame-Based Filtering: The backend invokes a Python script that processes
the video at a rate of one frame per second. For each frame, the script checks for the
presence of at least five players and the football pitch. If both conditions are met, the
corresponding second is marked for inclusion, and its frames are retained for further anal-
ysis.
Event Detection and Refinement: The filtered video segments are then analyzed for
specific key events, particularly instances where the ball is detected inside the penalty
area. When such an event is found, the script extracts a short clip consisting of five
seconds before and five seconds after the event timestamp.
Highlight Compilation: All extracted short clips are concatenated into a single high-
light video. This compilation forms a coherent summary of the most significant moments
in the match, based on predefined detection criteria.
Result Delivery to Frontend: Once the highlight video is generated, it is sent back
to the frontend. The user interface then provides a download link, allowing the user to

specialized object detection models—YOLOv10 for player, referee, goalkeeper, and ball
detection, and YOLOv8 for pitch and keypoint detection.
The project covered the full machine learning pipeline—from data collection, annota-
tion, and preprocessing to model training, evaluation, and deployment. Using annotated
datasets and data augmentation techniques, the models were trained and evaluated on
precision, recall, and mean Average Precision (mAP). The results confirmed the effective-
ness of our approach, achieving strong detection performance across relevant classes.
The final system was deployed within a user-friendly web application, where users can
upload match videos and receive automatically generated highlight reels. This end-to-end
pipeline demonstrated how modern AI techniques can streamline sports content creation
and reduce human effort without sacrificing quality or accuracy.
Perspectives
While the system has proven its capability, several improvements and extensions can be
pursued in the future:
• Real-Time Processing: Currently, the system operates offline. A real-time or
near-real-time version could be developed, enabling immediate highlight generation
during live broadcasts.
• Event Type Classification: Future versions could integrate action recognition
models to classify detected events more precisely—distinguishing goals, fouls, saves,
offsides, or red cards.
