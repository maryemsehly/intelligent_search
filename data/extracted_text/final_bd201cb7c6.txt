General introduction
Financial prediction is usually a diﬀicult task as a result of its extremely volatile and dynamic.
Financial establishments and traders have created numerous proprietary models to do and beat
the market for themselves or their clients, however seldom has anyone achieved systematically
higher-than-average returns on investment. Recently, the quantity of online news has rocketed
which makes it exhausting for investors to gather all the newest data. As a result, any quite
automatic system has been implemented to support the investors. Researchers have turned to
techniques within the technology fields of massive big and artificial intelligence for stock value
prediction. These apply computational power to increase theories in mathematics and statistics.
Machine learning and deep learning algorithms use given data to “find out” the solution to a
given drawback.
In this paper, we create a platform for trading and stocks fluctuation monitoring using machine
learning in time series forecasting problems. As we focus on a specific machine learning technique
such as Linear Regression and Decision tree and Deep Learning techniques such as Long Short
Term Memory, Autoregressive integrated moving average (Arima), and Convolutional neural
networks (CNN). Our objective is to use these algorithms to predict stock prices at a given time
in the future. We input the historical data to the model, split it into train and test sets, and
compare the algorithms based on the evaluation metric(MSE, ADF, etc.).
The plan for this work is organized as follows:
• In the first chapter, we introduce the study and the planning that we will follow to
complete the entire project and the agile methodology we’re going to follow.
• In the second chapter, we propose an overview of the study context that we are working on
in this project. First, we introduce the concepts on which the Financial prediction depends.
Second, we specify the importance of artificial intelligence in the field of market prediction,
and some related works will be exposed later. Third, we study related approaches given
by current researchers followed by an overview of the methodology used.
• The third chapter is intended to expose the implementation of the proposed approach as
well as the experiments that were carried out to evaluate its performance and limitations.
First, we describe the prediction approaches and model deployment. Second, we present
the process of the application realization while illustrating diagrams outlining the project
behavior and scenario and development technologies.

was the first publicly-traded company. To raise capital, the company decided to sell stock and
pay dividends of the shares to investors. Then in 1611, the Amsterdam stock exchange was
created. For many years, the only trading activity on the exchange was trading shares of the

your money into local currency. Foreign exchange (or FX) refers to the foreign exchange market,
where investors can buy and sell currencies from all over the world. It is the world’s largest
financial market, but many individual investors have never stepped into it, partly because it is
highly speculative and complex.

1.2.3
Proposed solution
The evaluation of the existing solutions has enabled us to identify the main needs as well as
solutions currently on the market. To overcome these gaps, we propose in this work to develop
a web application for managing the project that combines all the necessary features for this
problem.
Our application, called “SMART INVESTMENT” includes these key features:
• User management: add, edit and delete (Admin, users, etc.),
• Stock and funds view management: add, edit and delete (Admin, customers, users, etc.).
• Explore an ever-expanding variety of stocks and cryptocurrencies.
• Get the required advisory from ML models within the platform
• Monitor the latest market updates and exchange variations.
• All of these actions are linked to a notification and mailing system to make it easier to
communicate and disseminate information.

project management frameworks. Some, like Scrum, follow a more rigid, structured methodol-
ogy. Others, like Kanban, are easier to introduce and implement on top of existing processes.

planning at the beginning of the project, Agile methodologies are open to changing requirements
over time and encourage constant feedback from the end-users. Cross-functional teams work
on iterations of a product over a period of time, and this work is organized into a backlog that
is prioritized based on business or customer value. The goal of each iteration is to produce a
working product. Agile evolved from different lightweight software approaches in the 1990s and
is a response to some project managers’ dislike of the rigid, linear Waterfall methodology. It
focuses on flexibility, continuous improvement, and speed.

What is Kanban?
Kanban was inspired by the Toyota Production System and Lean Manufacturing. Different
teams would create a card (or Kanban) to communicate that they had the extra capacity and
were ready to pull more materials. Because all requests for parts were pulled from the order,
Kanban is sometimes called the “pull system.” These same ideas apply to software teams and
IT projects today.
In this context, development work-in-progress (WIP) takes the place of
inventory, and new work can only be added when there is an “empty space” on the team’s
visual Kanban board. Kanban matches the amount of WIP to the team’s capacity, improving
flexibility, transparency, and output. Kanban’s visual nature offers a unique advantage when
implementing Agile. The Kanban board is easy to learn and understand, it improves the flow
of work, and minimizes cycle time.
The advantages of Kanban include:
• Increases flexibility: Kanban is an evolving, fluid model. There are no set phase durations
and priorities are reevaluated as new information comes in.
• Reduces waste: Kanban revolves around reducing waste, ensuring that teams don’t spend
time doing work that isn’t needed or doing the wrong kind of work.
• Easy to understand: The visual nature of Kanban helps to make it incredibly intuitive
and easy to learn. The team doesn’t need to learn a completely new methodology, and
Kanban can be easily implemented on top of other systems in place.

subset of the metrics discussed in this post to have a concrete evaluation of your models.[38]
These metrics are grouped into different categories based on the ML model/application they
are mostly used for, and cover the popular metrics used in the following problems:
• Classification Metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC, AUC, etc.)
• Regression Metrics (MSE, MAE)
• Deep Learning Related Metrics (Inception score, Frechet Inception distance)
As a side note, it is also worth mentioning that metric is different from loss function. Loss
functions are functions that show a measure of the model performance and are used to train
a machine learning model (using some kind of optimization) and are usually differentiable
in the model’s parameters.
On the other hand, metrics are used to monitor and measure
the performance of a model (during training, and test), and do not need to be differentiable.
However if for some tasks the performance metric is differentiable, it can be used both as a loss
function (perhaps with some regularizations added to it), and a metric, such as MSE.

3.1.7
Limitation and Future enhancement
One of the limitations of this study is that we only looked at daily stock price data. As a result,
our parameters were calculated daily. This is different from actual stock funds and quantitative
trading institutions, which study price data at a far more granular level, on the order of minutes
or even seconds. We can further this study by looking at intra-day trading data in addition to
closing price data. [40]
By observing intraday trends, we can create more robust models that capitalize on sudden
changes in momentum during intra-day trading. Individual stocks often have price swings that
last only for a couple of minutes, which an intra-day model can analyze, and then use to predict
price direction in the next few seconds or minutes. This is how such financial institutions make
trading profits. This research could’ve had a lot of enhancement as we can use inputs other than
historical data to predict Stock price. Some studies have already worked on this aspect but had
way worse results when working on a thing like tweets and news articles due to the complexity
of working with text data to this prediction. Stock prices can change quickly because too many
things can influence them and we can’t know them all and this is the reason why we can’t
predict it 100% accurately. The advancement of Artificial Intelligence and modern technology
will get us closer to making this prediction as accurate as possible but never getting to perfection.
In order to enhance the prediction models implemented, we propose to introduce them into a
Web application that allows users to view the results of predictions on Market data downloaded
a priory. An administrator will be responsible for managing the data on this application while
