General Introduction
General Introduction
Effective management of equestrian facilities requires coordinated handling of multiple
resources, including stables, horses, staff, and daily operational planning. Traditional
management approaches in this sector often rely on manual processes and fragmented
systems, leading to inefficiencies, communication gaps, and operational challenges that
impact both staff productivity and animal care quality.
Our project addresses these challenges by developing a comprehensive digital solution
that centralizes stable management operations. The initiative aims to create an inte-
grated platform that streamlines user management, planning coordination, and daily task
execution through modern web technologies and mobile-responsive design.
The project encompasses the design and implementation of a role-based management
system that supports real-time collaboration, offline functionality, and scalable archi-
tecture to accommodate facilities of varying sizes. Through careful analysis of existing
solutions and stakeholder requirements, this work proposes an innovative approach that
combines traditional web applications with progressive web app capabilities and digital
display integration.
This report presents the complete development journey, beginning with project context
and problem analysis, followed by the design methodology and technical implementation.
The subsequent chapters detail the system architecture, key functionalities, and develop-
ment outcomes, concluding with project evaluation, lessons learned, and recommendations
for future enhancements.

Criticisms of Existing Solutions in Stable Management

To evaluate the solutions discussed in the previous section, we established a set of practical

• Stable Secretary
– Criterion 1 : Outdated User Interface
The interface appears less modern and can be unintuitive for new users, po-
tentially impacting the overall user experience and efficiency.
– Criterion 2 : Limited Mobile Functionality
The mobile version of Stable Secretary does not offer full feature parity with
the desktop version, reducing usability for users who rely heavily on mobile
access.
– Criterion 3 : Steep Learning Curve
The platform offers a wide range of features, which may require time and
training to master, particularly for users with limited technical background.

1.3
Problematic
In this project focused on developing a stable management system, we observed that
many equestrian facilities struggle with disorganized data handling, inefficient coordi-
nation among staff, and limited access to horse-related information, especially across
multiple stables. These challenges often lead to miscommunication, missed tasks, and
suboptimal care and planning for horses. Our goal is to address these issues by creat-
ing a unified, role-based management platform that offers secure authorization levels for
different users—such as stable managers, staff, and administrators—while also ensuring
offline accessibility for seamless operation in remote or low-connectivity areas. Through
this solution, we aim to enhance operational efficiency, improve coordination of care and
training schedules, and provide valuable insights for better decision-making, ultimately
contributing to the health and performance of the horses and the smooth functioning of
the stables.

1.5.1
Scrum
Scrum is an Agile project management framework that supports the iterative and incre-
mental development of complex products. Originally designed for software development,
it is now widely used across various industries. Scrum promotes collaboration, flexibil-
ity, and continuous improvement by organizing work into time-boxed sprints, guided by
clearly defined roles and structured events. This approach helps teams adapt to changing
requirements while maintaining consistent progress and product quality.
1.5.2
Approach
The Agile methodology employs a flexible, iterative framework characterized by the fol-
lowing key elements:
• Divides the development process into short, time-boxed iterations known as sprints.
• Each sprint results in a functional and incrementally improved version of the prod-
uct.
• At the end of each sprint, the team conducts a retrospective to reflect and enhance
future performance.
• Based on Agile principles that prioritize customer satisfaction, regular delivery, di-
rect communication, and continuous refinement.
• Focuses on maximizing delivered value, embracing change, and promoting strong
collaboration among team members.

Modeling language
With the dominance of object-oriented programming in today’s software development
landscape, shifting from a functional to an object-oriented mindset can present notable
difficulties. This highlights the need for tools and methodologies that facilitate the design
and understanding of object-oriented systems. For our project, we opted to use the Unified
Modeling Language (UML) as the primary framework for representing and modeling the
system’s architecture.

The Unified Modeling Language (UML) is a standardized visual language used to
model the design of software systems. It helps developers and stakeholders represent sys-
tem structure and behavior through diagrams like class, use case, and sequence diagrams.
UML improves communication, system understanding, and design clarity.
Using conceptual modeling approaches such as UML in the development of informa-
tion systems enhances the understanding of application needs and allows for high-level
abstraction of both technical components and user interactions.
UML as a formal and standardized language:
• The formal and unified nature of UML contributes to more precise system defi-
nitions and improves the overall consistency of design.
• Its structure encourages developers to integrate modeling tools into their work-
flows.
• By adhering to a standardized format, UML minimizes design inconsistencies,
enhances reliability, and contributes to efficient system development.
UML as an effective communication tool:
• UML diagrams simplify the expression of complex system behaviors and struc-
tures, aiding in cross-functional communication.
• Its adaptability and wide applicability make it suitable for diverse industries and
development teams.
• UML supports the visualization, construction, and documentation of software
systems, making it an essential tool throughout the software lifecycle.
The decision to adopt UML as the primary modeling language for our project was
driven by its key strengths: its formal, standardized foundation and its effectiveness as
a communication tool. UML’s flexibility in modeling a wide range of system structures
made it an ideal choice. Its visual nature, supported by a variety of diagram types, allows

2.1.2
Non-functional requirements
To ensure the application is intuitive, efficient, and secure—particularly in protecting
users’ data, we have defined the following non-functional requirements:
A) Usability: The interface should be clean, intuitive, and easy to navigate for all
types of users.
B) Efficiency: The system must perform all operations effectively, responding promptly
to user inputs and requests.
C) Security: Sensitive data must be protected through secure authentication mecha-
nisms, including unique usernames and passwords.
D) Responsiveness: The application should offer near real-time processing to main-
tain a smooth user experience.
E) Scalability: The system should be designed with future enhancements in mind,
allowing for easy integration of new features.
F) Accessibility: The application must comply with accessibility standards to accom-
modate users with varying needs and abilities.
G) Reliability: The system must provide stable performance with minimal downtime,
ensuring consistent access and data handling.
H) Visual Comfort: The user interface should adopt a pleasant and ergonomic color
scheme to minimize visual fatigue.
