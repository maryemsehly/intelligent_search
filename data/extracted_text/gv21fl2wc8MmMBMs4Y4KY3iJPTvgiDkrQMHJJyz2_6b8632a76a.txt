General Introduction
Industry is changing with the integration of digital technologies into production, main-
tenance, supervision, and technical decision support. This change is associated with In-
dustry 4.0, where industrial systems are no longer observed only through manual control
or isolated equipment, but through connected environments capable of producing and
using operational data. In this context, data becomes useful for understanding the state
of equipment, following process behavior, and supporting technical decisions. Industrial
digitalization therefore responds to the limits of traditional supervision methods, espe-
cially when data is still collected manually, stored in separate documents, or checked only
during periodic inspections.
The Internet of Things contributes to this evolution by allowing data sources to be
connected to supervision systems. A sensor-based monitoring system makes it possible
to collect industrial parameters, organize them, and present them in a form that is easier
to interpret. The value of such systems is not limited to displaying measurements. Their
main interest is to provide a clearer view of the monitored environment and to help users
identify abnormal situations at the right time.
This is why real-time monitoring has
become an important requirement in industrial contexts, where delayed information may
reduce the usefulness of supervision and slow down the reaction of technical teams.
These needs are particularly visible in agro-food environments, where production and
storage activities may depend on operational parameters related to equipment behavior,
process stability, and product conditions. In such contexts, supervision supports main-
tenance, quality follow-up, and process continuity. However, the implementation of a
monitoring system must remain practical. The collected data must be organized, inter-
preted, and presented clearly, so that technicians can distinguish normal operation from
abnormal conditions without being faced with an unnecessarily complex interface.
This final year project is placed within this general framework of industrial digitaliza-
tion and connected supervision. It concerns the study and realization of a platform for
monitoring industrial and agro-food environments. The project aims to provide a cen-
tralized view of sensor-based information and to support the identification of abnormal
states in a monitored environment.
This report is organized into four chapters.
The first chapter, Preliminary Study,
presents the internship context, the problem statement, the existing solutions study, and
the proposed solution. The second chapter, State of the Art, introduces the concepts
related to industrial digitalization, IoT-based monitoring, supervision systems, commu-
nication principles, data management, and anomaly detection. The third chapter, Con-

Chapter 1. Preliminary Study
1.3
Project Presentation
The project entitled IoT Cooperative Monitor concerns the study and realization of a
monitoring system for industrial and agro-food machines. The objective is to provide a
centralized supervision system capable of presenting operational information and helping
technicians follow the state of monitored equipment.
The project is positioned within the context of monitoring operational parameters
of agro-food machines. These parameters may include temperature, vibration, electrical
current, weight, level, and machine state. Their observation helps technical users follow
equipment behavior, identify abnormal operation, and consult supervision information in
a structured way.
The platform is used by two types of users: the Administrator and the Technician. The
Administrator manages access to the platform and can consult supervision information
when needed. The Technician follows the monitored machines, checks the sensor readings,
observes the machine state, and uses alerts to help with maintenance actions.
1.3.1
Problem Statement
In many industrial and agro food environments, monitoring is still done through man-
ual readings, separate files, or isolated tools. These methods can be enough for simple fol-
low up, but they become limited when several operational parameters have to be checked
regularly.
The main difficulty is the lack of a centralized and timely view of machine states and
abnormal situations. When information about temperature, vibration, current, weight,
level, or equipment state is fragmented, users may detect problems late, compare values
with difficulty, or lose previous readings. This reduces the usefulness of monitoring and
can delay the reaction of technical teams.
The problem addressed by this project can therefore be expressed as follows: how can
industrial supervision information be centralized in order to help identify abnormal states
and provide a clear view of monitored machine parameters in an industrial and agro-food
context?

1.4
Existing Solutions Study and Critique
Before presenting the proposed solution, it is useful to study existing solutions used
for connected monitoring and industrial supervision. This study helps us understand the
role of these platforms, their advantages, and their limits.
1.4.1
Existing Solutions
Cloud IoT platforms are a common solution for connecting devices and managing data
at a large scale. AWS IoT Core, for example, provides services for device connection, mes-
sage exchange, and integration with cloud applications [1]. These platforms are useful for
large distributed systems, but they require cloud configuration, service management, and
specific technical knowledge that are not always necessary for a local academic platform.
ThingsBoard is another IoT platform used for device management, data visualization,
dashboards, and rule-based processing [2]. It offers many useful monitoring features and
can be used in different contexts.
However, using a complete existing platform may
reduce the engineering contribution of the project, because several supervision functions
are already provided by the tool.
SCADA-oriented supervision systems are widely used in industrial environments. They
are designed to monitor machines, production lines, and industrial processes, often with
alarm management and control functions [3]. These systems are robust, but they are
generally heavier, more specialized, and more adapted to industrial infrastructure than
to an academic software implementation.
Node-RED-based local monitoring is also used in many IoT contexts because it allows
visual data flows to be created and connected to external services [4]. It is useful for

1.5
Proposed Solution
The proposed solution is a modular monitoring platform for industrial and agro food
machines. It follows a general supervision chain composed of data acquisition, commu-
nication, processing, storage, visualization, and alerting. Each part has a specific role in
the monitoring process.
The platform receives operational data related to monitored parameters such as tem-
perature, vibration, current, weight, level, and machine state. It organizes this informa-
tion, stores the useful records, and displays them through a supervision interface. Its
purpose is to give technicians a centralized view of the monitored machines instead of

2.2
Industry 4.0 and Industrial IoT Monitoring
Industry 4.0 refers to the digital transformation of industrial systems through con-
nected equipment, data exchange, automation, and supervision.
It allows industrial
activity to be observed through digital information instead of relying only on manual
follow-up or isolated machines [5, 6].
Industrial IoT applies the principles of the Internet of Things to industrial environ-
ments. It connects equipment, sensors, and software services in order to collect operational
data and support monitoring, maintenance, and technical decision making [7, 8]. In this
context, monitoring platforms are used to transform measurements produced by machines,
sensors, or connected data sources into readable information for technical users.

IoT System Architecture
An IoT system is generally organized into layers. The perception layer contains the
sensors or data sources. The communication layer transfers the collected information.
The processing and storage layer organizes the data. The application layer presents the
information to users [7].

Industrial monitoring consists in observing equipment, processes, or environmental
conditions in order to understand their state. In industrial and agro-food environments,
monitored information may concern temperature, vibration, electrical behavior, weight,
level, or other operational indicators.
Real-time supervision means that recent information is made available quickly enough
to support technical reaction. This requirement is important in industrial contexts be-
cause late information may reduce the value of monitoring when an abnormal state occurs.
Several works on industrial IoT monitoring show that real-time data acquisition, web-
based visualization, and alert mechanisms are commonly used to improve the follow-up
of industrial processes and machine conditions [9, 10].
A supervision system must therefore provide current values, historical views, and clear
status information. The objective is not only to collect measurements, but also to make
them usable by technicians who need to observe machine behavior and react when the
monitored state becomes abnormal.
2.5
Communication Protocols for IoT Monitoring
Communication protocols define how information is exchanged between devices, ser-
vices, and applications. In a monitoring system, the selected communication approach
must support repeated data exchange and clear routing of information.
HTTP, WebSocket, and MQTT are common communication approaches in connected
systems. HTTP is suitable for classical web requests. WebSocket is useful for live updates
between a server and a web interface. MQTT follows a publish-subscribe model and is
widely used for IoT telemetry because it is lightweight and adapted to machine-to-machine
communication [11].

2.7
Data Acquisition and Storage
Data acquisition is the step that allows information to be collected from the monitored
environment. In IoT monitoring, this information usually comes from sensors or connected
data sources and is then transmitted to software services for processing and visualization.
The acquisition stage must keep the meaning of the collected informations, their origin,
and their time of collection.
Data storage is also necessary because supervision does not only deal with the latest
value. Historical information helps users consult previous states, follow the evolution of
monitored parameters, and keep traces of abnormal situations. In IoT and industrial mon-
itoring systems, storage may be based on relational databases, time-series databases, or
cloud storage depending on the volume of data, query needs, and deployment constraints
[13, 14].
Relational databases remain useful when the system needs structured data, relations
between entities, user accounts, alerts, and historical records. PostgreSQL is an example
of an open-source relational database management system that supports structured stor-
age and SQL queries [15]. Time-series databases are also frequently studied for sensor
data because measurements are commonly associated with timestamps and continuous
evolution over time [13].
In an industrial monitoring context, the choice of storage technology must therefore
consider both the structure of the information and the way it will be consulted later. User
accounts, alerts, machine information, and historical readings require clear organization
so that the supervision platform can keep traces and support later analysis.
2.8
Diagnostic and Anomaly Detection
In an industrial monitoring system, collected data becomes useful when it helps users
understand the state of the monitored environment. Diagnostic methods are used to inter-
pret measurements, identify abnormal behavior, and support technical decision making.
They help transform raw values into information that can be understood and used by
technical users.
Anomaly detection consists in identifying observations that differ from an expected
behavior [16].
In industrial supervision, an abnormal situation may be linked to an
excessive value, a sudden variation, or a combination of several indicators. The diagnostic
approach may rely on explicit rules, thresholds, statistical analysis, semantic knowledge,

maturity of the system [17, 18].
Rule-based diagnostic methods are often used when the monitored process is known

General Conclusion
This final year project focused on the study and realization of a monitoring platform
for industrial and agro-food environments. The work started from the observation that
manual follow-up and scattered supervision information can limit the ability of techni-
cal users to observe machine states, consult previous readings, and react to abnormal
situations in a timely manner. The proposed solution was therefore oriented toward the
centralization of operational parameters such as temperature, vibration, electrical current,
weight, level, machine state, and alert information.
The preliminary study defined the project context, the host organization, the problem
statement, and the proposed solution. The state of the art then presented the technical
concepts related to Industry 4.0, IoT-based monitoring, real-time supervision, communi-
cation protocols, web dashboards, data storage, and diagnostic methods. After that, the
conceptual study specified the functional and non-functional requirements, identified the
main actors, and modeled the system through architecture and UML diagrams.
The realization part was carried out as a software implementation based on a local
Dockerized environment. The monitoring chain connects a Node-RED telemetry source,
Mosquitto MQTT communication, a FastAPI backend, PostgreSQL storage, a React dash-
board, and an SMTP notification service. The implemented platform allows the Admin-
istrator to manage technician accounts and allows the Technician to consult monitoring
values, historical charts, diagnostic information, alert history, and email notifications re-
lated to warning or critical states.
The functional tests confirmed the coherence of the implemented chain. They verified
service execution, telemetry transmission through MQTT, backend reception, database
persistence, live dashboard display, diagnostic information, technician management, ac-
count restriction, alert history, and email notification. These tests show that the platform
achieves the main objectives defined at the beginning of the project in a controlled local
environment.
However, the realized work remains a software demonstration and not an industrial
deployment. The telemetry source is simulated, the diagnostic logic is rule-based, and the
thresholds are defined for demonstration and engineering-oriented testing. A complete
industrial deployment would require calibrated physical sensors, connection to real ma-
chines, long-term data collection, security hardening, and validation under real operating
conditions.
As perspectives, the platform can be extended by connecting real industrial sensors
or controller data sources to the same MQTT-based monitoring chain.
Further work

operational feedback, and the collection of larger historical datasets to support more
advanced decision-support methods. These extensions would strengthen the transition
from a local monitoring implementation toward a more complete industrial supervision
