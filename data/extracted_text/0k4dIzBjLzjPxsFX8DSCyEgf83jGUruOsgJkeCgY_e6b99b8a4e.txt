Abstract
Keywords: Sales Forecasting, Salesforce, Einstein Analytics, XGBoost, Machine Learning,
Data Automation, CRM, Automotive Industry This report documents a four-month internship
at DRAXLMAIER Group, focused on developing a comprehensive sales forecasting and ana-
lytics solution using Salesforce. The project aimed to enhance decision-making capabilities by
predicting future sales trends with high accuracy, providing customizable analytics dashboards,
and automating critical sales processes. The implementation followed a Scrum methodology,
structured in four sprints: requirements analysis and planning, data preparation and model de-
velopment, visualization and automation, and testing and deployment. The solution leveraged
both Salesforce Einstein’s native forecasting capabilities and custom Python models (primarily
XGBoost) to create a hybrid approach that improved prediction accuracy. A significant innova-
tion during the internship was the development of a Data Mapping Automation Tool that reduced
manual processes from four hours to five minutes, eliminating errors and improving data consis-
tency. The project delivered interactive dashboards that visualize forecasts and key sales metrics,
along with automated workflows to alert stakeholders about critical changes in projected sales.
The final solution demonstrated significant business value by improving forecast accuracy, en-
abling data-driven decision-making, and saving substantial time through automation. Technical
achievements include successful integration between Salesforce and external Python models, de-
velopment of intuitive user interfaces, and establishment of a scalable architecture for future
enhancements.
Keywords: Sales Forecasting, Salesforce, Einstein Analytics, XGBoost, Machine Learning,
Data Automation, CRM, Automotive Industry

GENERAL INTRODUCTION
In today’s rapidly evolving automotive industry, the ability to accurately forecast sales and
analyze market trends has become a critical competitive advantage. For global suppliers like
DRAXLMAIER Group, optimizing sales processes and making data-driven decisions can signifi-
cantly impact business performance, resource allocation, and strategic planning. This internship
project addresses these challenges by developing a robust sales forecasting and analytics solu-
tion that leverages modern technologies and artificial intelligence.
0.1
Project Overview and Context
This internship report documents a four-month project conducted at DRAXLMAIER Group, a
global automotive supplier specializing in interior systems, electrical systems, and battery solu-
tions. The project focused on building an integrated sales forecasting and analytics system using
Salesforce Sales Cloud as the foundation, enhanced with Einstein Analytics for visualization
and custom Python models for advanced predictions. Prior to this project, DRAXLMAIER relied
primarily on manual forecasting methods using spreadsheets, which were time-consuming, error-
prone, and lacked the accuracy needed for strategic decision-making. Sales managers struggled
to identify trends quickly, and the absence of real-time data hindered their ability to respond to
market changes. Additionally, the lack of integration between various data sources created in-
formation silos that further complicated the forecasting process. The automotive industry faces
unique challenges in sales forecasting due to long sales cycles, complex supply chains, and the
highly technical nature of products. These factors make accurate predictions particularly valu-
able but also more difficult to achieve. By implementing a solution that combines Salesforce’s
native capabilities with custom machine learning models, this project aimed to address these
industry-specific challenges while providing actionable insights for business leaders.

0.2
Objectives of the Internship
The primary goal of this internship was to develop a comprehensive sales forecasting and analyt-
ics solution that would improve DRAXLMAIER’s ability to predict future sales trends and make
informed business decisions. Specifically, the project aimed to achieve the following objectives:
• • Accurate Sales Forecasting : Utilize advanced forecasting techniques, including Sales-
force Einstein and custom Python models, to predict future sales with high accuracy and
reliability
1. Customizable Analytics: Create interactive dashboards that allow stakeholders to track per-
formance across key metrics, including revenue, region, and team performance.
2. Enhanced Decision-Making: Provide actionable insights derived from predictive models to
support strategic planning, resource allocation, and sales strategy optimization.
3. Process Automation: Streamline repetitive tasks using Salesforce Flow to reduce manual
effort, minimize errors, and improve productivity.
4. Knowledge Development: Acquire expertise in Salesforce technologies, machine learning,
and data visualization techniques that could be applied to future projects within DRAXL-
MAIER.
5. Documentation and Knowledge Transfer: Create comprehensive documentation to ensure
the solution’s sustainability and facilitate knowledge transfer to other team members.
Throughout the internship, these objectives were pursued using a Scrum-based methodology,
with work divided into four monthly sprints, each delivering incremental value toward the final
solution.

1.1
Introduction
This internship report documents a comprehensive four-month project conducted at DRÄXL-
MAIER Group, focusing on the development of an AI-powered sales forecasting and analytics
solution. The project aimed to transform the company’s manual forecasting processes by lever-
aging modern technologies, specifically combining Salesforce’s native capabilities with custom
machine learning models to create a hybrid approach that addresses industry-specific chal-
lenges while providing actionable insights for business leaders.The internship, completed be-

system that achieved 85% prediction accuracy, automated critical sales processes, and delivered
substantial time savings for the sales organization. This report details the journey from initial
requirements gathering through final deployment, highlighting the technical innovations, chal-
lenges overcome, and business value delivered.

1.2.2
Topic Presentation
Sales Forecasting in the Automotive Supply Industry
Sales forecasting in the automotive supplier context presents unique challenges that differen-
tiate it from other industries. At DRÄXLMAIER, sales opportunities typically represent potential
contracts for providing electrical systems, interior components, or battery solutions to vehicle
manufacturers. These opportunities are characterized by:
1. Extended Sales Cycles: Negotiations often span 6-18 months due to the complexity of
automotive projects
2. High-Value Contracts: Individual opportunities can represent millions of euros in potential
revenue
3. Technical Complexity: Products require extensive customization and engineering collabo-
ration
4. Competitive Bidding: Most opportunities involve competing against other global suppliers
5. Multi-Stakeholder Decision Making: Involves technical, commercial, and strategic decision-
makers
The project aimed to address these complexities by developing an intelligent forecasting sys-
tem that could:
• Predict the probability of winning or losing sales opportunities with high accuracy
• Provide early warning signals for at-risk opportunities
• Enable data-driven resource allocation and strategic planning
• Automate routine forecasting tasks to free up time for strategic activities

1.6.2
Five Reasons Why Scrum is Ideal for Developers
1. Iterative Development and Continuous Feedback
Scrum’s iterative approach allowed for regular demonstrations of working features to stake-
holders. This was particularly valuable for the AI models, where early feedback on prediction
accuracy helped refine the feature selection and model parameters. Each sprint delivered a po-
tentially shippable increment, ensuring continuous value delivery.
2. Flexibility to Adapt to Changing Requirements
The automotive industry’s dynamic nature meant requirements could evolve. Scrum’s frame-
work allowed us to accommodate new insights, such as the need for the Data ID Standardization
Tool discovered during Sprint 2, without disrupting the overall project timeline.
3. Focus on Delivering Business Value
Each sprint was oriented around delivering specific business value. For example, Sprint
2 focused on achieving the core forecasting capability, ensuring that even if the project was
interrupted, DRÄXLMAIER would have a functional prediction system.
4. Enhanced Communication and Collaboration
Daily stand-ups and regular sprint events fostered strong communication between technical
development and business stakeholders. This was crucial for understanding the nuances of au-
tomotive sales cycles and incorporating domain knowledge into the models.
5. Risk Mitigation Through Early Detection

Salesforce Terminology:
– Einstein Prediction Builder: Salesforce’s no-code tool for building custom AI
models
– Einstein Analytics: Salesforce’s advanced analytics and visualization platform
(now Tableau CRM)

– Lightning Component: Reusable interface elements in Salesforce Lightning Ex-

– Custom Object: User-defined data table in Salesforce
– Apex: Salesforce’s proprietary programming language
– Change Set: Package of customizations for deployment between Salesforce en-
vironments
Machine Learning Terminology:
– XGBoost: Extreme Gradient Boosting, an efficient implementation of gradient
boosted decision trees
– Feature Engineering: Process of creating new variables from existing data
– Hyperparameter Tuning: Process of optimizing model configuration settings
– Cross-Validation: Technique for assessing model performance on multiple data
subsets
– Precision: Proportion of positive identifications that were actually correct
– Recall: Proportion of actual positives that were correctly identified
– AUC-ROC: Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve, measuring
model discrimination ability
– MAPE: Mean Absolute Percentage Error, a measure of forecast accuracy
Sales Forecasting Terminology:
– Opportunity: Potential sales deal in progress
– Pipeline: Collection of sales opportunities in various stages
– Win Rate: Percentage of opportunities that are successfully closed
– Sales Velocity: Measure of how quickly deals move through the sales process
– Lead Source: Origin of a sales opportunity
