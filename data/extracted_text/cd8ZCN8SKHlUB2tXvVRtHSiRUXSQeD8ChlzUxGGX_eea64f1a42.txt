General Introduction
Agriculture is experiencing a profound transformation driven by the convergence of ad-
vanced technologies and sustainable practices. Among the most promising innovations
is the hydroponic greenhouse system, which enables high-eﬀiciency crop production with
minimal use of land and water — a vital response to global challenges such as food insecu-
rity, climate change, and dwindling natural resources. Widely adopted in North America,
Europe, and East Asia, soilless farming represents a paradigm shift toward intelligent,
data-centric agriculture. The integration of Internet of Things (IoT) devices, Artificial
Intelligence (AI), and computer vision technologies allows for continuous environmental
monitoring, automated control of climate conditions, and precise nutrient management.
These technologies empower systems to detect diseases early, assess plant health, and
make informed decisions through predictive analytics. As a result, modern greenhouses
have evolved into self-regulating ecosystems capable of maintaining ideal growing environ-
ments throughout the year. This fusion of AI and IoT positions hydroponic agriculture
as a scalable and resilient model for the future of food production. In Tunisia, hydroponic
farming remains largely in the developmental stage, with limited commercial deploy-
ment. Although there is a growing interest in applying smart technologies, their use is
predominantly confined to academic research and experimental projects. For example,
some studies have explored the use of photovoltaic-powered greenhouses that combine
solar energy, sensor networks, and AI algorithms to optimize productivity and resource
eﬀiciency.

1.4.6
Global Architecture
1.4.6.1
Global System Architecture
The architecture of the Plantini smart hydroponic greenhouse system is structured to
deliver an automated, scalable, and intelligent solution for modern agriculture.
It is
designed around a modular, four-layer model: physical, network, middleware, and
application.
• Physical Layer: This layer comprises all hardware components responsible for en-
vironmental sensing and control. It includes ESP32 and ESP32-CAM boards con-
nected to sensors (temperature, humidity, water level, and pH), actuators (pumps
and LED lights), and an access control mechanism using face recognition. These
components operate autonomously to gather real-time data and interact with the
environment.
• Network Layer: The physical devices communicate over a secure Wi-Fi network,
allowing seamless transmission of sensor data and command signals. Each ESP32
module is connected to the same local network, enabling distributed control while
maintaining synchronization with a central backend.
• Middleware Layer: This layer acts as the intelligence core of the system.
It
integrates Supabase as a backend-as-a-service platform, providing secure authenti-
cation, real-time database management, and cloud-based file storage. It also serves
as the bridge between IoT data and AI inference, linking the system to external
models for rat detection, leaf disease diagnosis, and access verification.
• Application Layer: At the user level, the system is controlled via a cross-platform
Flutter mobile application and a dedicated web dashboard. The Plantini mobile app
allows farmers to monitor real-time stats, receive alerts, manage greenhouse sections,
and interact with a marketplace. The web dashboard, reserved for administrators,
provides interfaces for product management, order processing, and user oversight.
This layered architecture ensures that each component of the system is both indepen-
dent and interoperable. It provides high modularity for future upgrades, scalability for
additional greenhouse sectors, and robustness for real-time agricultural automation (see
Figure 1.2).

1.4.7.1
Technologies and Tools
To implement the proposed Plantini smart hydroponic solution, a range of hardware and
software technologies was adopted across the system’s physical, network, middleware, and
application layers. Each tool was carefully selected based on its performance, compati-
bility with IoT and AI applications, ease of integration, and availability in the Tunisian
context.
• Hardware:
– ESP32 and ESP32-CAM: Chosen for their built-in Wi-Fi, low cost, and com-
munity support. ESP32 offers robust GPIO and analog/digital interfacing,
while ESP32-CAM integrates a camera module, eliminating the need for an
external processor for image acquisition.
– Sensors: DHT22 was selected over alternatives like DHT11 due to its superior
accuracy and broader sensing range. The analog water level sensor was se-
lected for its simplicity and real-time fluid level detection. The pH sensor was
chosen for its proven compatibility with ESP32 and its critical role in nutrient
monitoring in hydroponics.
– Actuators: 12V water pumps were used for their ability to deliver suﬀicient
pressure in small-scale hydroponic systems. The 5V door lock was chosen for
its direct compatibility with microcontroller outputs and ease of installation.
LED grow lights were selected because of their low energy consumption and
spectral suitability for plant growth.
– Camera Modules: The built-in OV2640 camera in ESP32-CAM was chosen
due to its affordability and suitability for embedded video tasks such as live
streaming, face recognition, and image capture for AI inference.
• Software:
– Mobile & Web Applications: Flutter was selected for its ability to develop both
mobile and web interfaces from a single codebase, saving time and ensuring
consistency. Dart, the language behind Flutter, provides excellent performance
and native compilation support.
– Machine Learning Models:
∗YOLOv5: Chosen for its balance between speed and accuracy. Com-
pared to earlier YOLO versions or alternatives like SSD or Faster R-CNN,
YOLOv5 offers better GPU utilization and faster inference — ideal for
real-time rat detection.
∗CNN: Selected for leaf disease classification due to its success in image
classification tasks. It provides a lightweight and eﬀicient model structure,
suitable for server-based inference with limited training data.
∗face_recognition Python Library: Used for its simplicity and pre-trained
support. It eliminates the need for complex neural network design while
still offering accurate face encoding and comparison.
– Cloud Services: Supabase was chosen over Firebase for its open-source nature,
SQL compatibility, and easier integration with RESTful APIs and real-time
subscriptions — while offering a free tier that fits the academic use case.

Node Descriptions and Responsibilities
• Environment Node (ESP32 + DHT22 + LED Lamp):
Responsible for ambient condition monitoring, this node uses a DHT22 sensor
to capture real-time temperature and humidity. It also controls an LED grow
light, turning it on or off based on environmental needs or timer schedules.
• Water Tank Node (ESP32 + Water Level Sensor + pH Sensor +
Water Pump):
This node tracks the pH level and water level in the nutrient reservoir. If the
water level drops below a predefined threshold, the ESP32 activates the pump
to refill the tank. The pH readings help assess solution quality.
• Access Control Node (ESP32-CAM + Door Lock):
Positioned at the system entrance, this node controls access using face recog-
nition. The ESP32-CAM captures a facial image and sends it for processing.
If the face matches an authorized identity, the door lock is automatically re-
leased.
• AI and Streaming Node (ESP32-CAM):
This node captures continuous video feeds and sends them to a processing
server. AI models running externally analyze the stream for rat presence and
leaf disease symptoms. Detected events are logged and notifications are sent
via Supabase.
This architecture isolates responsibilities across distributed microcontrollers, enabling par-
allel operations and ensuring that each subsystem can be independently upgraded, tested,
or replaced as needed.

Glossary
AI: Artificial Intelligence – The simulation of human intelligence in machines that
can learn, reason, and make decisions.
API: Application Programming Interface – A set of functions and protocols that
allow different software applications to communicate with each other.
CNN: Convolutional Neural Network – A deep learning algorithm primarily used
for analyzing visual imagery, such as leaf disease detection in this project.
DHT22: A digital sensor used to measure ambient temperature and humidity, commonly
used in environmental monitoring systems.
ESP32: A low-cost, low-power microcontroller with built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth used
for IoT applications.
ESP32-CAM: A variant of the ESP32 microcontroller equipped with an OV2640 camera
module for capturing images or streaming video.
Figma: A web-based user interface and UX design tool used to create mockups and UI
layouts.
Flutter: An open-source UI toolkit from Google used for developing cross-platform mo-
bile and web applications with a single codebase, written in Dart.
GPIO: General Purpose Input/Output – Digital signal pins on microcontrollers
used to control or read values from sensors and actuators.
HTTP: Hypertext Transfer Protocol – The foundation of data communication on
the web.
IDE: Integrated Development Environment – A software suite (e.g., Arduino IDE,
VS Code) that combines code editing, compiling, and debugging tools.
IoT: Internet of Things – A network of interconnected devices capable of collecting
and exchanging data in real time.
NFT: Nutrient Film Technique – A hydroponic growing method where a thin film of
nutrient solution flows over the plant roots.
pH Sensor: A sensor used to measure the acidity or alkalinity of the nutrient solution
in a hydroponic system.
QR Code: Quick Response Code – A type of matrix barcode used for secure identi-
fication and login to the system.

Abstract
This final year project presents the design and implementation of an intelligent, fully au-
tomated hydroponic farming system integrated with a cross-platform mobile application.
The objective is to empower users to deploy and manage hydroponic farms in any location
while remotely monitoring and controlling operations in real time.
The system architecture is centered around multiple ESP32 microcontrollers and
ESP32-CAM modules, coupled with DHT22 temperature and humidity sensors, pH sen-
sors, water level sensors, automated valves, and water pumps. Communication between
hardware and backend is achieved through HTTP protocol, with data storage, authenti-
cation, and analytics handled by a Supabase-powered cloud service.
The mobile application developed using the Flutter framework offers a user-friendly
interface for real-time environmental monitoring, actuator control, alert notifications,
growth tracking, and access to a dedicated agricultural marketplace for product exchange
between users and administrators.
Computer vision features have also been implemented for real-time rat detection and
leaf disease classification using ESP32-CAM video capture and server-side AI inference.
The entire system has been successfully tested, validated, and deployed in a functional
prototype.
Keywords: Hydroponics, IoT, ESP32, ESP32-CAM, Supabase, Flutter, Mobile App,
Web Dashboard, Smart Agriculture, Automation, Computer Vision, Artificial Intelligence,
YOLOv5, CNN, Face Recognition, Rat Detection, Leaf Disease Detection, Scrum.ٌۘ
