General Introduction
General Introduction
Workplace safety has become an increasingly important concern across various indus-
tries, particularly in fields that involve physical labor, heavy machinery, or exposure to
hazardous substances. As operational environments grow more complex and the volume
of collected data rises, organizations are finding new opportunities to apply emerging
technologies—especially Artificial Intelligence (AI)—to manage safety risks in a more
proactive and predictive manner.
This internship was conducted as part of a wider effort aimed at enhancing Health,
Safety, and Environment (HSE) practices using data-driven approaches. The primary
focus was to investigate how past safety incident records could be analyzed to forecast
potential hazards and recommend preventive strategies. The ultimate goal is to support
safer work conditions, minimize disruptions, and ensure regulatory compliance.
To approach this challenge methodically, the project adopted the CRISP-DM (Cross-
Industry Standard Process for Data Mining) framework. This industry-standard method-
ology outlines six essential stages: Business Understanding, Data Understanding, Data
Preparation, Modeling, Evaluation, and Deployment. Each phase ensures that the solu-
tion remains both technically robust and aligned with the organization’s operational needs.
This report provides a comprehensive overview of each step taken throughout the
project—from defining the business problem and exploring the dataset to developing pre-
dictive models and extracting actionable insights. It also reflects on technical obstacles
and real-world considerations associated with integrating AI into workplace safety sys-
tems.
Ultimately, this project contributes to the evolving field of predictive analytics in
occupational safety, demonstrating how AI can move safety strategies beyond reactive
measures to a more forward-thinking, preventative approach.

Chapter 1: Project Context
Chapter 1
Project Context
Introduction
In the context of industrial operations, especially within the automotive sector, main-
taining a safe working environment is not only a legal obligation but also a cornerstone
of sustainable productivity. At large-scale manufacturers such as the DRÄXLMAIER
Group, traditional safety management practices are often limited to post-incident inter-
ventions. As industry shifts toward digital transformation through Industry 4.0, Artificial
Intelligence (AI) offers promising tools for anticipating and preventing occupational acci-
dents. This chapter introduces the hosting company, outlines the motivation and frame-
work of the project, details the current limitations of the safety management process,
and presents the rationale behind adopting the CRISP-DM methodology to guide our
predictive modeling workflow.
1.1
Host Organization: DRÄXLMAIER Group
1.1.1
Company Overview
Established in 1958, the DRÄXLMAIER Group [1] is a leading first-tier supplier to pre-
mium automotive brands worldwide. Its core competencies include:
• High-voltage and onboard electrical systems
• Battery technologies for electric mobility
• Customized interior modules (e.g., door panels, consoles)
• Complex wiring harnesses and vehicle electrical architectures

Abstract
This final-year project was carried out within DRÄXLMAIER Group Sousse and fo-
cuses on the development of an intelligent system for predicting occupational accidents and
recommending appropriate safety measures. The main objective is to provide a proactive
tool that supports workplace risk management through artificial intelligence.
The approach followed the CRISP-DM methodology, guiding the project through
data understanding, preprocessing, modeling, and deployment phases.
After cleaning
and transforming historical incident data, a predictive model was trained to classify the
most likely accident type based on a worker’s attributes. The solution includes a backend
developed in Flask to serve predictions via an API, and a ReactJS-based frontend for
user interaction. MongoDB was used for data storage, ensuring the system can evolve
and integrate future incidents for retraining and analysis. A dashboard was also created
for real-time visualization and decision support.
