GENERAL INTRODUTION
General Introduction
R
egardless of personal viewpoints, technology has greatly changed how we communicate
and maintain connections in recent years. It has transformed from a basic data-sharing
process into a deeply interconnected digital landscape, where numerous critical services and
business functions now rely on digital systems.
This swift advancement has also been accompanied by an increase in cybercrime. Cur-
rently, organizations, regardless of their size, face a growing array of cyber threats. The
growth of cloud computing, interconnected systems, and Internet of Things (IoT) devices
has greatly broadened the opportunities for potential attacks. Consequently, cybersecurity
has evolved from being merely an IT issue to becoming an essential element of an organiza-
tion’s ability to withstand and recover from challenges.
The increasing prevalence of cyber threats emphasizes the necessity for more effective
approaches to identify, analyze, and reduce risks. If vulnerabilities are not discovered and
resolved promptly, organizations could face significant repercussions, including data breaches
or ransomware attacks.
Nonetheless, numerous organizations continue to depend on manual or somewhat auto-
mated methods for managing vulnerabilities and deploying patches. These approaches tend
to be slow, prone to mistakes, and challenging to expand in today’s intricate settings. This
establishes a definite requirement for automated systems that can identify vulnerabilities,
evaluate their seriousness, and implement corrective measures with limited human involve-
ment.
In summary, although advancements in technology have enhanced security measures,
they have also led to more intricate cyberattacks and increased complexity in systems. This
blend has resulted in manual patch management becoming progressively ineffective and more
unreliable, highlighting the significance of implementing automation.

GENERAL INTRODUTION
In the current landscape, it is no longer feasible to stay uninvolved, and relying exclusively
on human efforts to oversee countless vulnerabilities is not realistic.
In fulfillment of the requirements for earning a Bachelor’s degree in Computer Engineer-
ing, with a focus on Systems and Network Engineering at the Higher Institute of Computer
Science of Mahdia, this project aims to integrate Falcon Spotlight with an automated patch
management system.

2.1 DevOps Approach
DevOps is a combination of the terms Development and Operations. It is a collaborative
approach that aims to unify software development and IT operations in order to improve
eﬀiciency and communication. It also seeks to automate and streamline the software delivery
process through Continuous Integration and Continuous Deployment (CI/CD).
By integrating development and operations, DevOps enables faster innovation, reduces
time-to-market, and supports agile methodologies.
As shown in Figure 10, the DevOps lifecycle is represented as an infinite loop.
The left side represents the Development phase, which consists of:
• Plan: defining requirements and objectives.
• Code: writing and developing the application.
• Build: compiling and packaging the code.
• Test: verifying functionality and detecting issues.
On the other hand, the right side represents the Operations phase, which consists of:
• Release: preparing the application for deployment.
• Deploy: delivering the application to production.
• Operate: managing and maintaining the system.
• Monitor: tracking performance and detecting problems.

2.2 DevSecOps Approach
DevSecOps is an evolution of the DevOps approach that integrates security practices directly
into every stage of the software development lifecycle. It not only enhances application secu-
rity without affecting the speed and agility of software delivery, but also promotes a culture
of shared responsibility where security is a fundamental component of both the development
and operations processes.

2.3 Containerization
Containerization is the process of encapsulating an application and its dependencies into
an isolated container that runs consistently across different computing environments.
It
represents a paradigm shift in software deployment and has reshaped the way applications
are developed, tested, and deployed, particularly when integrated with CI/CD pipelines.
Docker is the leading platform for containerization. It provides a powerful and eﬀicient
way to build, package, and deploy applications within isolated containers. In addition, by
using Docker images, applications can be easily replicated and deployed with minimal con-
figuration. Furthermore, Docker Hub and private registries provide centralized storage and
distribution of container images.
2.4 Continuous Integration / Continuous Deployment
(CI/CD)
2.4.1
Continuous Integration (CI)
Continuous Integration (CI) refers to the practice of regularly merging code changes into
a common repository. Every integration initiates automated builds and tests to ensure the
code is validated.
Continuous Integration (CI) enhances the quality and reliability of code by enabling
ongoing validation.
This approach assists developers in identifying bugs and integration
problems at an early stage of the development process.
2.4.2
Continuous Deployment (CD)
Continuous Deployment (CD) refers to the automation of the process involved in releasing
and deploying applications.
It minimizes the need for manual involvement, resulting in
quicker and more dependable deployments. In addition, it provides rollback mechanisms to
address any failures that may occur.
2.5 Automation Playbooks
As organizations strive for faster delivery and higher quality, automation has become an
essential approach. Automation performs tasks with minimal human intervention, making

security checks, patch management, and compliance validation procedures.

Pre-Patching Phase
Before deploying security patches, a pre-patching phase is automatically executed to prepare
the target systems for remediation operations and ensure recover-ability in case of deployment
failure. The main objective of the pre-patching phase is to create a reliable rollback point
before any modification is applied to the infrastructure.
5.4.4
Pre-Patching Linux
The Linux pre-patch playbook is responsible for collecting and preserving critical recovery
information before package deployment. Rather than performing a full system restoration,
this workflow is designed to support lightweight and targeted rollback operations. Further-
more, this playbook is based on the principle of restoring only impacted services, packages,
and configurations.
The pre-patch includes:
• Backup of /etc configuration files.
• Capture of installed package versions for downgrade operations.

5.4.10
Rollback Phase
After the health check phase makes its decision in the remediation pipeline, it determines
whether the system remains stable after patch deployment.
If the health check considers a host as unhealthy, the system automatically triggers the
rollback mechanism through AWX.
Rollback triggers include:
• Service failure after patching.
• Critical system service crash.
• Abnormal system state after update.
During Rollback, only the packages identified as causing instability are downgraded to
their previous versions. Packages that remain healthy and not affecting the system stability
are preserved at their updated versions.

General Conclusion
Our end-of-studies project entitled “Automated Vulnerability and Patch Management with
Falcon Spotlight” implemented a complete workflow for managing and deploying updates for
operating systems such as Linux and Windows. The main objective was to enhance system
security, stability, and performance by structuring and automating the patch management
process.
Throughout the work, several key stages were achieved, such as vulnerability analysis,
pre-patching preparation, and health checks. Deployment procedures were designed for both
Linux and Windows environments, ensuring reliability across different systems.
This project highlighted the importance of automation in modern IT operations, as it
reduces human error, improves operational eﬀiciency, and reduces system downtime during
maintenance operations. It also exposes the details of the whole process, including the total
number of machines, CVE numbers, machines that needed rollback, and which ones had
successful patching or failed, with full host details providing complete visibility into the
patching process.
As for future perspectives, the project remains extensible and offers areas for improve-
ment, such as automation of patch management for installed software and applications across
all managed hosts, and artificial intelligence predictive rollback that can predict the failure
before it happen by detecting abnormal during the patch deployment.
