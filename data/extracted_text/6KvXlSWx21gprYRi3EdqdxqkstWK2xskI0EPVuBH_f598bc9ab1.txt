General Introduction
Cheating during academic examinations remains a serious issue, with significant im-
plications for fairness, academic integrity, and the value of educational qualifications.
With the increased reliance on remote exams and the growing sophistication of cheating
methods, traditional supervision techniques are no longer sufficient to detect and prevent
dishonest behavior.
In Tunisia, as in many parts of the world, universities and educational institutions
are seeking modern, AI-powered solutions to ensure fair and secure testing environments.
Recent statistics and academic reports have highlighted the increased use of unauthori-
zed tools and collaborative cheating methods, making it crucial to adopt proactive and
intelligent monitoring systems.
To address these challenges, our final year project proposes the development of an
intelligent invigilation system called Exam Eye. This system aims to assist exam supervi-
sors in detecting cheating attempts in real time using computer vision techniques. Exam
Eye uses a local webcam to monitor student behavior and detect anomalies such as the
exchange of materials, suspicious head movements. Once the exam session is launched by
the supervisor via a secure dashboard, the system begins local video analysis and alerts
the supervisor to any detected suspicious events, accompanied by visual evidence.
This solution offers a lightweight, local, and privacy-aware alternative to traditional
proctoring tools. It ensures the integrity of the examination process while respecting
students’ data by keeping all processing and storage on the local machine.
This report is organized as follows :
— In the first chapter, “Project Study”, we introduce the general context and the
host organization. We then present the initial research phase including existing
solutions, the objectives of the project, and the chosen methodology.
— The second chapter, “Sprint 0 : Preparatory Phase”, covers the requirement
analysis and system specification. This includes functional and non-functional re-

1.4.1
Problematic
The main objective of this project is to design an intelligent assistance system that
supports exam supervisors in detecting fraudulent behaviors during examinations. Rather
than replacing human oversight, this system is intended to act as a complementary tool
that enhances the supervisors’ ability to identify cheating, especially under challenging
conditions.
In recent years, academic fraud has become a serious concern, undermining the cre-
dibility of academic degrees. A 2019 study conducted at Tunisia’s four Higher Institutes
of Sport and Physical Education revealed that 86.1 % of surveyed students admitted
to cheating during their university education [9]. This widespread misconduct not only
weakens the academic environment but also has long-term socioeconomic consequences,
as graduates who lack essential skills may enter the workforce unprepared, potentially
affecting national economic performance
While supervisors strive to maintain discipline and fairness during exams, factors
such as fatigue, limited focus, and increased student numbers can hinder their ability to
detect all forms of cheating. Moreover, the lack of advanced technological tools limits
the effectiveness of traditional monitoring practices. This project aims to bridge that gap
by providing a technological aid that works alongside human supervisors, improving the
overall integrity and reliability of the examination process.

1.4.2
Project Goals
This project aims to implement a smart assistance system designed to detect chea-
ting during examinations. The proposed solution leverages the capabilities of computer
vision to support human supervisors by providing real-time alerts through an interactive
dashboard.
By integrating cutting-edge AI technologies, this system enhances the ability to mo-
nitor exam environments effectively, especially in situations where human oversight alone
may be limited due to fatigue, distraction, or large class sizes. The primary goal is not to

Proposed solution
After a thorough analysis of the existing approaches, we propose a hybrid solution that
combines the strengths of human supervision and CCTV-based monitoring, enhanced by
artificial intelligence. The proposed system, named ExamEye, is designed to serve as
a smart assistant for supervisors, enabling real-time detection of suspicious behaviors
during examination sessions.The solution is built around two main components :
1.6.1
Real-Time Detection
The core feature of the solution is its real-time fraud detection capability. This is
achieved through the integration of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) object detection
algorithm, which is trained on a custom dataset to track and monitor student movements
during exams. When a potentially suspicious action is detected, the system captures a
screenshot of the scene. This information is then transmitted to the supervisor through
a centralized dashboard interface.

interface, the supervisor can start exam sessions, monitor real-time alerts, and view
evidence whenever suspicious behavior is detected. The supervisor can then either
confirm a case of fraud or dismiss the alert based on the evidence provided.
By combining AI-driven surveillance with human oversight, ExamEye enhances the
reliability and efficiency of exam supervision while supporting fairness and transparency

This section outlines the development of detection algorithms designed for identifying

4.2.2.1
Dataset
• Collection : To ensure that the models can effectively detect various types of
cheating behaviors—such as looking around, using electronic devices, and exchanging
papers—images were collected from several public datasets. Among these, datasets from
platforms like Kaggle [7] and Roboflow [16] were utilized, containing images captured in
diverse cheating scenarios. Additionally, videos simulating exam situations were recorded
to further enrich the dataset and improve model robustness.
• Data Preprocessing and Cleaning :
After data collection, a rigorous data cleaning process was carried out to ensure high-
quality input for training the algorithm. This involved a manual review of all collected
images and videos to eliminate duplicates and remove corrupted files.
In parallel, the annotations across all datasets were standardized and corrected. The
annotation process followed a binary classification scheme where :
Class ID 0 :
corresponds to "Cheating" behavior (e.g., using a device, looking around, or exchanging
papers),
Class ID 1 :
corresponds to "Not Cheating" (normal behavior during an exam).

General Conclusion
This final-year project was conducted in collaboration with Atomic IT as part of
the requirements for obtaining a Bachelor’s degree in Computer Science at the Higher
Institute of Computer Science of Mahdia (ISIMA). The primary goal was to design and
develop a smart and reliable assistance system for detecting academic fraud during exams
using real-time video analysis.
The resulting prototype, named Exam Eye, employs a surveillance camera to monitor
examination rooms and identify suspicious behavior such as paper exchanges or abnormal
movements. When such actions are detected, the system sends immediate alerts to the
supervising teacher, thereby reinforcing exam integrity and fairness.
The project followed the Kanban agile methodology and was structured across incre-
mental development cycles. It covered all key stages of the software engineering pro-
cess—requirements gathering, architecture design, implementation, and testing. Core
functionalities implemented include real-time monitoring, motion detection, and auto-
mated alerting.
This internship provided a valuable opportunity to apply theoretical knowledge in
a practical setting, particularly in the fields of software development and AI-assisted
surveillance. Despite certain constraints, such as limited time and hardware availability,
the project successfully delivered a working prototype that meets its intended objectives.
While the current prototype of Exam Eye meets its primary objectives, several
enhancements could significantly improve its performance and usability. Future work
could involve integrating facial recognition to verify student identity, refining the detection
algorithm to reduce false positives, and expanding the system’s capabilities with advanced
AI models like behavior pattern analysis.
Overall, this experience was enriching on both technical and personal levels. It fos-
tered the development of essential skills in software engineering, problem-solving, and
project management. The work accomplished is expected to lay a strong foundation for
future advancements in intelligent exam monitoring systems, and I hope it meets the
expectations of both the Atomic IT team and the evaluation committee.
