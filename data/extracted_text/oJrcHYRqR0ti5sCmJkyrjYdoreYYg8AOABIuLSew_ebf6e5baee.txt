1.1
Introduction
Industrial production systems rely on the continuous and reliable operation of machines. Any
unexpected equipment failure can propagate through the production chain, leading to down-
time, delivery delays, increased operational costs, and in some cases, safety risks. Consequently,
ensuring the reliability of machines and detecting faults early are key priorities in modern in-
dustrial environments.
In many facilities, maintenance is still based on traditional strategies. Corrective maintenance
involves repairing equipment after a failure has occurred, while preventive maintenance relies on
scheduled interventions at fixed time intervals. Although these approaches are widely adopted,
they present important limitations. Corrective maintenance leads to unplanned downtime and
high emergency repair costs, while preventive maintenance can result in unnecessary interven-
tions and does not guarantee that failures will be avoided between maintenance cycles.
With the emergence of the Internet of Things (IoT) and data-driven technologies, industrial
maintenance is progressively evolving toward continuous monitoring systems. These systems
enable real-time acquisition of physical parameters such as temperature, vibration, and acoustic
signals directly from machines. Combined with data processing and anomaly detection tech-
niques, this approach allows for the earlier identification of abnormal behaviour, supporting
predictive maintenance strategies.
In this context, our project aims to design and implement an IoT-based machine supervision
system named ELA (Evolution through Learning and Awareness). With ELA we’re
proposing a solution that integrates embedded sensing, real-time data communication, and
intelligent processing to improve machine monitoring and support early fault detection in in-
dustrial environments.
The development process was organised using the SCRUM agile methodology, allowing
iterative implementation of system components including hardware integration, communication
infrastructure, data processing pipelines, and monitoring interfaces.

1.3
Project Context
The main objective of the project is to design a modular supervision platform capable of
continuously monitoring machine behaviour and detecting abnormal operating conditions at
an early stage. The system is based on real-time data acquisition, transmission, storage, and
analysis.
The monitored parameters were selected based on their relevance to machine health assessment:
• Temperature sensor:used to detect overheating and thermal stress
• Vibration sensor : associated with mechanical wear and imbalance
• Sound level sensor: often correlated with abnormal mechanical friction or faults
Data collected from sensors is transmitted to a central monitoring infrastructure where it is
stored and processed for visualization and analysis. When abnormal patterns are detected,
alert mechanisms are triggered to notify operators.
From a hardware perspective, the system is based on an ESP32 microcontroller, selected
for its integrated Wi-Fi capabilities and suitability for IoT applications. It is interfaced with
multiple sensors and integrated into a custom-designed PCB to improve reliability, maintain-
ability, and mechanical robustness. A magnetic mounting system was adopted to facilitate
non-invasive installation on industrial machines.

1.4
Problem Statement
Despite technological advancements, many industrial environments still rely on maintenance
strategies that do not exploit real-time machine data. This results in limited visibility over
equipment condition and delayed fault detection.
Corrective maintenance is reactive by nature, as it intervenes only after a failure occurs. While
simple to implement, it leads to production interruptions and high operational costs due to
unplanned downtime.
Preventive maintenance improves reliability by scheduling regular servicing operations. How-
ever, since interventions are time-based rather than condition-based, they may occur unneces-
sarily or fail to prevent faults that arise between scheduled inspections.
In addition, manual monitoring approaches provide limited and discontinuous insight into ma-
chine behaviour. Small anomalies often remain undetected until they evolve into critical failures.
The core problem addressed in this project can therefore be formulated as follows:
How can an IoT-based supervision system continuously monitor industrial equipment and
enable early detection of abnormal behaviour before failure occurs?
1.5
Analysis of Existing Maintenance Approaches
Industrial maintenance strategies can be broadly classified into two main categories:
• Corrective Maintenance: intervention occurs after failure detection. Although it min-
imizes planned maintenance effort, it introduces significant operational risks due to un-
expected downtime.
• Preventive Maintenance: maintenance operations are scheduled periodically regard-
less of actual equipment condition.
While improving overall reliability, this approach
may lead to unnecessary interventions and does not guarantee failure prevention between
cycles.
Both approaches suffer from a common limitation: the lack of continuous, data-driven moni-
toring of machine health.

1.6.2
Overview of the ELA System
To address the identified limitations,we designed and implemented an IoT-based supervision
system named ELA (Evolution through Learning and Awareness) . The system com-
bines embedded sensing, IoT communication, real-time data processing, and anomaly detection
mechanisms to enable continuous machine monitoring. The overall architecture includes the
following components:
• ESP32-based embedded data acquisition unit
• Temperature, vibration, and sound sensors
• MQTT communication layer using Mosquitto broker
• Node-RED for data processing and workflow orchestration
• InfluxDB for time-series data storage
• Grafana for real-time visualization dashboards
• Alerting and notification mechanisms
• AI-assisted anomaly detection integrated into Node-RED workflows
Sensor data is continuously collected by the embedded unit and transmitted via MQTT to
the backend infrastructure. Node-RED processes incoming data streams, stores them in the
time-series database, and triggers visualization and alerting workflows.
In addition to threshold-based monitoring, we integrated an anomaly detection module as a
proof of concept to enhance predictive maintenance capabilities.

robustness while preserving the functionality validated on the breadboard prototype.
Although the PCB was not physically manufactured, its design represents a complete hardware
blueprint for future iterations of the ELA system.

MQTT Communication Architecture
The communication between the embedded system and the backend infrastructure is based
on the MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport) protocol, which is widely used in IoT
systems due to its lightweight nature and publish/subscribe model.
System Components
The MQTT architecture is composed of the following elements:
• ESP32 (Publisher): Sends sensor data (temperature, vibration, sound) to the MQTT
broker.
• MQTT Broker: Acts as an intermediary responsible for receiving, filtering, and dis-


===== PAGE 56 =====

CHAPTER 4. SPRINT 2: COMMUNICATION INFRASTRUCTURE AND REAL-TIME
MONITORING
50
tributing messages.
• Node-RED (Subscriber): Processes incoming messages and routes them to different
services.
• Storage and Visualization Layer: InfluxDB stores the data, while Grafana visualizes
it in real time.
Data Flow
The data flow in the system follows a publish/subscribe mechanism:
1. The ESP32 collects sensor data from connected modules.
2. Data is published to specific MQTT topics.
3. The MQTT broker receives and forwards messages to subscribed services.
4. Node-RED processes and transforms the data.
5. Processed data is stored in InfluxDB and visualized using Grafana.
Design Rationale
MQTT was selected due to its low bandwidth consumption, simplicity, and suitability for
constrained IoT devices. Its publish/subscribe architecture ensures scalability and decouples
the embedded system from backend services, allowing independent evolution of each component.

Machine Learning-Based Detection
An unsupervised Isolation Forest model is used to detect abnormal patterns without requir-

approach.
An unsupervised Isolation Forest model is used to detect abnormal operating conditions without
requiring labeled failure data. The model is trained using historical sensor measurements stored
in a sliding buffer for each monitored machine.
For every incoming telemetry message, the temperature, vibration, and sound measurements
are combined into a feature vector and evaluated by the trained model. The Isolation For-
est computes an anomaly score representing the deviation of the current machine state from
previously learned normal operating behavior.
The resulting anomaly score is normalized and compared against predefined warning and critical
thresholds. Higher scores indicate a greater probability of abnormal machine behavior. This
approach enables the detection of both sudden failures and gradual performance degradation
that may not be captured by conventional threshold-based monitoring techniques.

After the training phase, each new telemetry sample is evaluated by the model. The Isola-

6.5
Project Limitations
Despite the successful implementation of the ELA platform, several limitations were identified
during the project development and testing phases. The main limitations include:
• Limited access to industrial infrastructure: The project was developed within an
academic environment, which did not allow access to real industrial machinery operating
under production conditions. Consequently, the system was validated on a small-scale
prototype and simulated fault scenarios rather than on actual industrial equipment.
• Budget constraints for industrial-grade hardware: The selection of sensors and
components (e.g., ESP32, low-cost MEMS microphone, and accelerometer) was guided
by cost constraints typical of academic projects. Industrial-grade sensors with higher
precision, calibration stability, and certification were outside the available budget.
• Time constraints within academic planning: The project was developed within a
fixed academic timeframe defined by the PFE schedule. This limited the possibility of
long-term data collection, multi-season testing, and extended model training for the AI
component.
• Restricted availability of real failure datasets: Reliable industrial failure datasets
(machine breakdown logs, labeled degradation signals) were not accessible. Therefore,
anomaly detection relied on live collected data and controlled threshold-based simulation.
• Deployment environment limitations: Testing was performed in a controlled lab-
oratory environment. Environmental factors such as electromagnetic interference, dust,
humidity, and mechanical vibration diversity found in industrial plants could not be fully
reproduced.
• Single-node validation scope: Due to scope and resource limitations, the system was
validated on a single IoT node. While the architecture is scalable by design, large-scale
multi-machine deployment was beyond the project perimeter.
These limitations affected the scale of validation but did not prevent the successful implemen-
tation of the proposed monitoring solution.

GENERAL CONCLUSION
This report presented the design, implementation, and evaluation of a complete intelligent
predictive maintenance system for industrial monitoring applications. The objective was to
move beyond traditional monitoring approaches and build a unified architecture capable of
sensing, communicating, analyzing, and predicting machine behavior in real time.
The developed system was built progressively across multiple stages. The first phase focused
on establishing a reliable IoT monitoring infrastructure based on an ESP32 embedded node
equipped with vibration, temperature, and acoustic sensors. This data acquisition layer was
integrated with an MQTT communication protocol and a Dockerized backend composed of
Node-RED, InfluxDB, and Grafana, ensuring real-time data flow, storage, and visualization.
In the second phase, the system was enhanced with intelligent features, including a rule-based
alert mechanism and an AI-driven anomaly detection module integrated within Node-RED.
This addition allowed the system not only to react to abnormal conditions but also to identify
early signs of mechanical degradation through behavioral analysis of sensor data. The use of
an Isolation Forest-based approach provided a lightweight yet effective predictive capability
suitable for edge and near-real-time environments.
Parallel to the software development, a custom PCB was designed using KiCad and validated
using Fusion 360. This transition from breadboard prototyping to a dedicated embedded board
significantly improved system stability, reduced wiring complexity, and enhanced signal relia-
bility. In addition, a mobile application developed with Flutter extended system accessibility
by enabling real-time remote monitoring and instant alert notifications.
The final evaluation demonstrated that the system operates as a fully integrated industrial-
grade architecture. All components from hardware sensing to cloud like visualization—work in
synchronization through a unified MQTT-based communication structure. The system achieved
low-latency performance, stable data transmission, and effective anomaly detection with pre-
dictive capability, confirming its suitability for real-world monitoring scenarios.
